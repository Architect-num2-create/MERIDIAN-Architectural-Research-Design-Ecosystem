# MERIDIAN — Design DNA

The DNA is not the list of things MERIDIAN contains.

It is the small number of behaviours that keep appearing when the architecture changes.

My current reading is this:

### 1. One thing becomes many

The project does not seem comfortable with a single, untouched mass.

It splits.

Then it aggregates.

Multiplicity is therefore not just a visual condition. It may be a consequence of how the building is generated.

### 2. Many things still need a centre

The parts are not allowed to become an arbitrary collection.

There is a centre.

The centre gives hierarchy to the field around it.

This is why "many domes" is a weak description of the project. The more important question is:

**how does a dominant centre organize differentiated others?**

### 3. The mass is continuously interrupted

Split.

Open.

Cut.

Expose.

Void is not the opposite of form here. It is one of the ways form becomes legible.

The architecture seems to gain identity from the relationship between what remains solid and what is deliberately removed.

### 4. Geometry changes state

The project repeatedly moves between conditions:

whole → divided  
flat → layered  
closed → opened  
linear → radial  
continuous → stepped  
hidden → exposed

This may be more important than any particular shape.

### 5. Vertical difference creates hierarchy

The project does not treat height as a single roofline.

There are layers, steps and different vertical characters.

The centre can rise.

The surrounding elements can differentiate.

The section becomes part of the composition rather than a technical cut made afterwards.

### 6. Radiality is structural to the language

Radial thinking appears around the centre, in dome studies, in curvature and in pattern.

But it should not become "everything must be radial".

The useful question is:

**what does radial organization solve here?**

If it only makes something look ornate, it is not DNA.

### 7. Striation may be deeper than texture

The striated dome study suggests a repeated layered condition.

That could become:

surface  
structure  
light  
section  
movement  
or geometry.

Until one of these is proven architecturally, "striated" should remain an open gene, not a style.

### 8. History enters through behaviour

Saint Peter does not need to become Saint Peter.

Saint Basil does not need to become Saint Basil.

Iranian architecture does not need to become an Iranian façade.

The useful inheritance is the architectural behaviour underneath:

axis, hierarchy, multiplicity, threshold, proportion, depth, structure, pattern, light.

Then the behaviour is changed.

That is where MERIDIAN can become its own architecture.

---

## The deeper DNA

If I had to reduce the project brutally:

**centre + difference + transformation**

But even that is not enough.

The real test is whether every major formal decision can eventually answer:

**what changed, why did it change, and what did that change do to space?**

If a form cannot answer that, it may still be beautiful.

It just has not earned its place in MERIDIAN yet.
