## 01 — Purpose

This document defines the research architecture, epistemic rules, source handling,
analytical procedures, extraction methods, transformation logic, and documentation
protocol used throughout the MERIDIAN project.

MERIDIAN does not treat historical architecture as a catalogue of forms to be
reproduced.

Historical sources are treated as structured knowledge systems from which
architectural principles, spatial relations, formal operations, cultural
conditions, material logics, symbolic systems, and environmental strategies may
be extracted, examined, transformed, and recombined.

---

## 02 — Central Research Question

How can heterogeneous historical architectural knowledge be transformed into a
new contemporary architectural language without reducing history to visual
reproduction?

---

## 03 — Research Position

MERIDIAN operates on the following proposition:

> History is not a finished form.
> History is a field of operations.

The project therefore investigates how historical knowledge can move through a
controlled sequence:

OBSERVATION
→ DOCUMENTATION
→ DECOMPOSITION
→ EXTRACTION
→ CLASSIFICATION
→ COMPARISON
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ SYNTHESIS
→ EMERGENCE

The final architecture is not intended to reproduce its sources.
It is intended to demonstrate what can emerge when their underlying systems are
subjected to architectural transformation.

## 05 — Source Classes

Every significant source should be classified before being used.

### S1 — Primary Architectural Sources

Original buildings, drawings, plans, sections, archival documents,
inscriptions, archaeological evidence, measured surveys, photographs, and
other direct architectural evidence.

### S2 — Scholarly Sources

Peer-reviewed papers, academic books, dissertations, institutional research,
and established scholarly publications.

### S3 — Institutional Sources

UNESCO, museums, archives, universities, heritage organizations,
architectural institutions, and other authoritative bodies.

### S4 — Secondary Interpretations

Critical essays, architectural criticism, historiography, professional
publications, and expert interpretations.

### S5 — Visual / Documentary Sources

Photographs, drawings, diagrams, documentaries, databases, and visual archives.

### S6 — Project-Generated Evidence

MERIDIAN's own diagrams, experiments, models, drawings, rejected iterations,
calculations, observations, and design tests.

---

## 06 — Evidence Status

Claims must not all be treated as equally certain.

Use the following epistemic tags:

**F1 — Established Fact**  
Directly supported by reliable evidence.

**F2 — Scholarly Consensus**  
Widely supported interpretation within relevant scholarship.

**F3 — Plausible Scholarly Interpretation**  
A defensible interpretation but not universally established.

**I1 — Authorial Interpretation**  
An interpretation developed within MERIDIAN.

**H1 — Working Hypothesis**  
A proposition currently being investigated.

**U1 — Unknown**  
Insufficient evidence currently available.

**R1 — Rejected / Disputed**  
Previously considered or commonly stated but rejected, contradicted,
or insufficiently supported within the project.

## 08 — Extraction

MERIDIAN distinguishes between:

### Historical Object

What physically exists.

### Historical Logic

The organizational principle behind the object.

### Architectural Operation

What the designer can do with that logic.

### Transformation

How the operation changes when transferred into a new context.

### Emergent Result

What new architectural condition appears after transformation.

The project must avoid jumping directly from:

HISTORICAL OBJECT → NEW FORM

without documenting the intermediate reasoning.

## 10 — Transformation Protocol

A historical element may enter the design system only after its underlying
logic has been examined.

Transformation may occur through:

- Abstraction
- Mutation
- Scaling
- Fragmentation
- Aggregation
- Repetition
- Inversion
- Rotation
- Compression
- Expansion
- Substitution
- Hybridization
- Reorganization
- Structural reinterpretation
- Spatial reinterpretation
- Material reinterpretation
- Environmental reinterpretation

No transformation should be considered valid merely because it produces an
interesting visual result.

SYNTHESIS

---

## 12 — Negative Knowledge

Rejected possibilities are part of the research record.

The project should preserve:

- Rejected sources
- Rejected interpretations
- Failed transformations
- Unsuccessful combinations
- Contradictory evidence
- Design dead ends
- Unresolved questions
- Technical limitations
- Methodological failures

A failed experiment is not automatically wasted work.

It may define the boundary of the design system.

---

## 13 — Traceability

Important architectural decisions should be traceable.

Whenever possible:

DESIGN DECISION
→ RESEARCH FINDING
→ SOURCE
→ EVIDENCE
→ INTERPRETATION
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ ARCHITECTURAL RESULT

This chain should remain recoverable throughout the project.

---

## 14 — Research Integrity

MERIDIAN must distinguish clearly between:

what is known,

what is inferred,

what is hypothesized,

what is designed,

and what is deliberately fictional or speculative.

No historical claim should be presented as fact merely because it supports the
architectural concept.

---

## 15 — Versioning

Research is versioned.

Major changes to methodology, interpretation, evidence classification, or
design logic should be documented rather than silently overwritten.

Version format:

MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH

Example:

0.1.0
0.2.0
1.0.0

## 17 — Open Questions

This protocol remains intentionally incomplete.

As MERIDIAN develops, new research instruments may be introduced when they
demonstrate genuine value to the project.

The methodology is therefore itself subject to transformation.

---

## 18 — Core Principle

> Do not reproduce the source.
> Decode the source.
> Do not imitate its appearance.
> Identify its operative logic.
> Do not stop at extraction.
> Transform.
> Do not stop at transformation.
> Synthesize.
> Do not assume the result.
> Investigate what emerges.
