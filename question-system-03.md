MERIDIAN — Question System

Dossier: Research / Investigation
ID: 03
Status: Foundation

---

A question in MERIDIAN is not a heading for a section of research.

It is a pressure placed on the project.

A useful question should be capable of changing what the project sees, accepts, rejects, or does next.

The questions below are therefore not intended to be answered once and closed. Some will become more precise. Some may split into smaller questions. Some may be rejected. Others may survive long enough to shape the architecture.

---

01 — The Historical Question

What, beyond visible form, actually constitutes the architectural intelligence of a historical work?

This asks what can be observed in a historical source before interpretation begins.

It includes spatial order, geometry, hierarchy, proportion, movement, structure, threshold, material, light, shadow, ornament, environmental response, and the relationships between them.

The purpose is not to produce a complete description.

It is to determine what is worth carrying forward.

---

02 — The Extraction Question

What can be extracted from a historical source without reducing it to a motif?

Not every recognizable characteristic deserves to become an architectural input.

The investigation must distinguish between:

- appearance
- relationship
- rule
- condition
- operation
- cultural or historical circumstance

The question is therefore not simply what exists, but what can survive abstraction without becoming meaningless.

---

03 — The Transformation Question

What changes when an extracted historical logic enters a different architectural condition?

Transformation should not be assumed to mean abstraction, distortion, fragmentation, or any other predetermined operation.

The operation itself has to be discovered and tested.

A transformation is useful only if the change can be understood in relation to both its source and its new condition.

---

04 — The Boundary Question

How much can a historical logic change before its relationship to the source becomes arbitrary?

And conversely:

How much can remain unchanged before transformation becomes reproduction?

This boundary cannot be fixed in advance.

It may differ between geometry, spatial organization, ornament, structure, material, light, or other architectural systems.

---

05 — The Evidence Question

What allows a transformation to be defended as more than a convincing design idea?

This requires separating:

what is historically established,
what is an interpretation,
what is inferred from comparison,
what is hypothesized,
and what is simply proposed by the designer.

The project should be able to show where a transformation came from without pretending that every design decision has a historical precedent.

---

06 — The Iranian Question

What can constitute an Iranian architectural position when reproduction of recognizable historical language is refused?

This question is deliberately open.

It does not assume that Iranian identity must be found in motifs, ornament, materials, proportions, symbols, or any other familiar category.

The research must discover whether continuity can exist through deeper architectural relationships instead.

---

07 — The Emergence Question

What can appear through transformation that was not explicitly present in the historical source?

If transformation only produces a more complicated version of what was already known, its generative value remains limited.

MERIDIAN therefore has to pay attention to the moment when transformed relationships begin to produce something that cannot simply be described as a historical reference.

That is where emergence becomes a research question rather than a claim.

---

08 — The Architectural Question

When does an interpretation become architecture?

A historical insight has not yet become an architectural result merely because it has been diagrammed.

The investigation must follow the point at which knowledge begins to affect:

space, mass, void, sequence, threshold, structure, surface, light, shadow, movement, perception, or another architectural condition.

---

09 — The Reversal Question

What can the architecture reveal back to the research?

The process is not expected to move permanently from history toward design.

A design experiment may expose a contradiction in the historical interpretation.

A spatial result may reveal that an extracted principle was misunderstood.

A failure may generate a better question than a successful iteration.

Research therefore remains open after transformation begins.

---

10 — The Final Question

The project ultimately returns to a question that cannot be settled by a single answer:

How can historical architectural intelligence become the material for a contemporary architecture without becoming either reproduction or arbitrary invention?

MERIDIAN does not require this question to disappear.

Its purpose is to make the project increasingly capable of answering it—and increasingly capable of recognizing when its current answer is insufficient.

---

Question Behaviour

Questions in MERIDIAN may:

OPEN — begin an investigation
NARROW — define a specific uncertainty
SPLIT — reveal several different problems
CHALLENGE — test an existing assumption
REVERSE — send the investigation back toward an earlier decision
CLOSE — become sufficiently resolved
REJECT — lose validity or prove unproductive
GENERATE — produce a new question

The Question System is therefore not a checklist.

It is a living structure that records where the project is still thinking.
