# 24 — FINAL ARCHITECTURAL SYNTHESIS

The final building should not feel like the place where every previous idea was finally included.

It should feel like most things disappeared, and the few things that remained became much stronger.

---

## Synthesis is reduction.

The project may begin with:

history,
mass,
cross,
centre,
radiality,
multiplicity,
domes,
striation,
threshold,
light,
structure,
material.

The final architecture does not need to show all of them equally.

Some become primary.
Some become secondary.
Some disappear completely.

That is not losing research.

That is designing.

---

## The layers should start talking to each other.

A formal decision should have a spatial consequence.

The spatial consequence may create a structural condition.

The structure may affect thickness.

Thickness may change the light.

Light may change how the centre is perceived.

That perception may change movement.

Now the project starts behaving like one building.

---

## This is where historical sources should disappear from the surface.

The final building should not need labels saying:

"this came from Saint Peter."

or:

"this is the Iranian part."

The sources have done their job if they changed the architecture.

The building should be able to stand without them.

That is the real test of transformation.

---

## There should still be tension.

A completely resolved building can become dead.

MERIDIAN probably needs some relationships that remain slightly unstable:

axis / radial  
one / many  
heavy / light  
closed / open  
historical / contemporary  
monumental / intimate

Not confusion.

Tension.

The building should hold both sides without becoming visually undecided.

---

## The final question

If every diagram and explanation disappeared tomorrow:

**What would still make this building recognizably MERIDIAN?**

Not the number of domes.

Not a particular ornament.

Not a historical quotation.

Something deeper should remain.

That is what the synthesis has to find.
