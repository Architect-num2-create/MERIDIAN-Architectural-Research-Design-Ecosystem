# 10 — RESEARCH MATRIX

The [Four-Source Matrix](../05_FOUR_SOURCE_MATRIX/) compares the four
territories to each other. This file holds the other cross-cutting
comparisons — the ones that cut through all four territories at once around
a single theme.

## The numbers matrix

Every load-bearing number in MERIDIAN, checked against its nearest
historical analogue, and labeled per the [Evidence System](../08_EVIDENCE_SYSTEM/).
This table exists so that no number in the project is ever ambiguous about
its own status.

| MERIDIAN number | Nearest historical analogue | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 7 domes (1 central + 6 peripheral) | Saint Basil's: 9 (stone), 7 (earlier wooden phase, different building) | D2 — Design Decision |
| 6 subordinate domes | Saint Peter's: Bramante planned 4, della Porta built 2, 0 built at rear | D2 — Design Decision |
| 5 Achaemenid-inspired columns | Apadana: 72; Throne Hall: ~100 | D2 — Design Decision |
| 185 courtyard columns | No direct analogue identified in any of the four territories | D2 — Design Decision, pending a stated internal derivation (module, rhythm, or count logic) — see [Research Gaps](../11_RESEARCH_GAPS/) |
| 3 stepped concentric dome levels | No architectural precedent identified; conceptually related to ziggurat massing | D2 — Design Decision |
| 2 convergent courtyard arms | Ilkhanid: 4 iwans; Achaemenid: 1 linear axis | D2 — Design Decision (transformation of two source geometries) |
| 5 : 2 dome pattern split (Saint Basil-style : Ilkhanid-style) | No direct analogue | D2 — Design Decision |
| 100 × 70 cm sheet, 5 cm frame | Studio brief requirement (dimensions), frame depth is authorial | Sheet size: brief requirement (F1); frame depth: D2 |

The pattern across this table is consistent and worth naming once, plainly:
none of MERIDIAN's own numbers are historical citations, and all of them are
legitimate design decisions once labeled as such. The only number in the
table that is not a MERIDIAN invention is the sheet size, which was set by
the studio brief, not chosen by the author.

## The claim-strength matrix

A second cross-cutting view: how strong is each of MERIDIAN's major
arguments, independent of which territory it touches?

| Argument | Strength | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Iranian double-shell priority (Soltaniyeh) | Strong | F2-grade historical priority, clearly documented |
| Four-source labor division avoids collage | Strong | Each source assigned a distinct architectural role (see [Four-Source Matrix](../05_FOUR_SOURCE_MATRIX/)) |
| Threshold-sequence as transferable operator | Moderate | Well documented in sources; MERIDIAN's specific application (two arms) not yet argued in Dossier II |
| Kinetic dome mechanism feasibility | Weak, by design | No precedent exists; explicitly held as H1/X1, not claimed as resolved |
| Reader-experience / cognitive engagement claims | Weak, currently | Asserted more than evidenced; see [Knowledge Limitations](../13_KNOWLEDGE_LIMITATIONS/) |

A weak rating is not a problem to hide — it is information the project needs
in order to know where not to overstate itself, especially in
[Dossier III — Paper](../../03_PAPER/).
