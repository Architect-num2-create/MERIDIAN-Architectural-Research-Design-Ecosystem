# MERIDIAN — Source Decoders

Historical sources should enter the project only after being taken apart.

The question is never:

"what should I copy?"

It is:

"what does this building know?"

## Saint Peter

The useful material is not the silhouette.

It is the way the building organizes:

axis,

crossing,

hierarchy,

central event,

scale,

movement,

and monumental sequence.

The project can take those relationships and make them behave differently.

A cruciform organization does not have to remain a cruciform plan.

The historical source is the beginning of a question.

## Saint Basil

Its value is partly in multiplicity.

Different volumes can belong to one composition without becoming identical.

There is also rhythm, geometric differentiation and chromatic intensity.

The danger is obvious:

many domes + strong colours = Saint Basil imitation.

That is exactly the level to avoid.

The deeper question is how difference is controlled.

## Ilkhanid / Iranian

Here the entrance becomes important.

Depth.

Threshold.

Layering.

Vault.

Geometry.

Light and shadow.

Surface and proportion.

The useful inheritance is spatial thickness.

An entrance can carry history through how it makes a person pass from outside to inside.

That is stronger than attaching an Iranian pattern to a wall.

## Achaemenid

The important material is not simply "columns with capitals".

There is a relationship between column, proportion, ceremonial scale, repetition and structural presence.

If a column is used, it should belong to the architecture before it announces its historical origin.

A column that exists only to say "Achaemenid" is a costume.

A column that changes span, scale, procession or structural reading is architecture.

---

## The decoder rule

Every source should pass through the same uncomfortable question:

**If I remove the appearance, what architectural knowledge is left?**

Then another:

**If I transform that knowledge, what changes in my building?**

And finally:

**Could the resulting condition exist without the historical source being recognizable?**

If yes, the source may have actually been transformed.

If no, we are probably still copying.
