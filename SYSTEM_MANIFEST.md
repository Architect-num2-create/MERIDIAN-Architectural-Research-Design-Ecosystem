00 — PURPOSE

This document defines the system-level architecture of MERIDIAN.

It does not describe the architectural project in detail, reproduce the
project manifesto, or function as a research dossier.

Its purpose is to define:

- what MERIDIAN is as a system
- how its major components relate to one another
- what constitutes its intellectual core
- how knowledge moves through the system
- which principles govern development
- how future documents, experiments, critiques and extensions should relate to
  the project

"PROJECT_MANIFEST.md" defines the identity and foundational context of the
project.

"SYSTEM_MANIFEST.md" defines the architecture and operating logic of the
ecosystem surrounding that project.

The distinction is intentional.

---

01 — SYSTEM DEFINITION

MERIDIAN is an architectural research and design ecosystem built around a
single architectural project and the knowledge generated through its
investigation, transformation and documentation.

The system treats architecture simultaneously as:

- artifact
- process
- evidence
- argument
- archive
- experiment
- interface
- knowledge structure
- future research substrate

The architectural object is therefore not isolated from its documentation.

The project and the system documenting it form a recursive relationship:

ARCHITECTURAL ARTIFACT
        ↓
OBSERVATION
        ↓
RESEARCH
        ↓
KNOWLEDGE
        ↓
TRANSFORMATION
        ↓
DESIGN
        ↓
EXPERIMENT
        ↓
ARCHITECTURAL RESULT
        ↓
NEW EVIDENCE
        ↺

MERIDIAN is therefore not intended to function as a static project archive.

It is a traceable system of architectural thought.

---

02 — SYSTEM OBJECTIVE

The primary objective is to investigate how historical architectural
knowledge can become active material for contemporary architectural
production without collapsing into visual reproduction or historical collage.

The system therefore seeks to make visible the chain between:

HISTORY
→ OBSERVATION
→ DECOMPOSITION
→ EXTRACTION
→ INTERPRETATION
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ SYNTHESIS
→ EMERGENCE
→ ARCHITECTURE

The system must preserve the distinction between what was:

- observed
- documented
- inferred
- interpreted
- hypothesized
- experimentally proposed
- designed
- rejected

This distinction is fundamental to the credibility of the ecosystem.

---

03 — CORE SYSTEM PRINCIPLE

The governing architectural principle is:

«Transform relationships, not appearances.»

Historical sources are therefore not treated primarily as collections of
visual motifs.

They are investigated as systems containing potentially transferable
relationships between:

- geometry
- space
- structure
- proportion
- circulation
- material
- light
- ornament
- construction
- environment
- symbolism
- ceremony
- cultural context

The desired output is not a recognizable mixture of historical identities.

The desired output is an emergent architectural system.

HISTORICAL INPUT

        ↓

        ↓

The five layers are not independent containers.

They form a recursive knowledge system.

                 ┌──────────────┐
                 │ RESEARCH │
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        ↓
                 ┌──────────────┐
                 │ DESIGN │
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        ↓
                 ┌──────────────┐
                 │ PAPER │
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        ↓
                 ┌──────────────┐
                 │ DARK FOREST │
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        ↓
                 ┌──────────────┐
                 │ CORE │
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        │
                        └──────────────→ NEW RESEARCH

The actual system behaves as:

RESEARCH ↔ DESIGN
DESIGN ↔ PAPER
PAPER ↔ DARK FOREST
DARK FOREST ↔ RESEARCH
CORE ↔ ALL LAYERS

The Core is therefore not simply the "last folder."

It is the connective infrastructure of the entire ecosystem.

07 — KNOWLEDGE FLOW

Knowledge within MERIDIAN should be traceable.

A significant architectural claim should ideally be capable of being traced
back through its genealogy.

The preferred chain is:

SOURCE
→ EVIDENCE
→ CLAIM
→ INTERPRETATION
→ QUESTION
→ HYPOTHESIS
→ DECISION
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ EXPERIMENT
→ RESULT

Not every design decision will possess every stage.

However, when a relationship is important, the system should make its
provenance reconstructable.

08 — EPISTEMIC STATUS

MERIDIAN distinguishes knowledge by epistemic status.

The following classification is normative:

Code| Status| Meaning
F1| Established Fact| Directly established by reliable evidence
F2| Scholarly Consensus| Supported by established scholarship
F3| Plausible Scholarly Interpretation| Defensible interpretation within scholarship
I1| Authorial Interpretation| Interpretation produced by the project
H1| Working Hypothesis| Proposition requiring further investigation
X1| Experimental Proposition| Deliberately tested or testable design proposition
U1| Unknown| Relevant but currently unresolved
R1| Rejected| Explicitly rejected after evaluation
D1| Design Decision| Adopted architectural decision

These labels should be used consistently across the research and design
infrastructure.

10 — DESIGN GRAMMAR

MERIDIAN seeks to progressively construct a design grammar.

The grammar is not:

- a visual style
- a catalog of motifs
- a formal recipe
- a replication algorithm

It is a description of relationships and operations.

Its conceptual structure is:

INPUT
→ VARIABLE
→ RELATION
→ OPERATOR
→ CONSTRAINT
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ TEST
→ SELECTION
→ SYNTHESIS
→ EMERGENCE

The grammar should remain generative rather than prescriptive.

It should explain how architectural intelligence operates without forcing all
future outputs to resemble MERIDIAN.

---

11 — OPERATOR PRIORITY

MERIDIAN privileges architectural operators over symbolic descriptions.

A metaphor may initiate investigation.

An operator must explain what happens.

For example:

METAPHOR:
"Yin-Yang"

OPERATIONAL TRANSLATION:
opposition
→ reciprocity
→ convergence
→ interdependence
→ spatial interaction

The system therefore asks:

«What does the reference cause the architecture to do?»

rather than:

«What does the reference supposedly represent?»

This principle applies to historical, cultural, symbolic and conceptual
references throughout the project.

---

12 — ARCHITECTURAL CENTRALITY

All system components remain subordinate to architecture.

Research is valuable when it changes or clarifies architectural
understanding.

Documentation is valuable when it preserves architectural intelligence.

Technology is valuable when it increases architectural capability,
traceability or accessibility.

Theory is valuable when it sharpens architectural reasoning.

Complexity without architectural consequence should be treated with caution.

The governing test is:

«What architectural consequence does this create?»

---

13 — EVIDENCE GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN must not convert uncertainty into false certainty.

Every significant claim should be evaluated according to:

1. What is known?
2. What is supported?
3. What is inferred?
4. What is interpreted?
5. What is hypothesized?
6. What remains unknown?
7. What evidence would change the conclusion?

Historical claims require source support.

Technical claims require technical support.

Scientific claims require scientific support.

Claims about cognition or neuroscience require appropriate empirical
literature.

Speculative architectural mechanisms must be explicitly identified as
speculative when they have not been technically demonstrated.

---

14 — TRANSFORMATION GOVERNANCE

Historical material should enter the design system through identifiable
operations.

The preferred transformation sequence is:

OBSERVE
→ DECOMPOSE
→ EXTRACT
→ ABSTRACT
→ TRANSFORM
→ TEST
→ RECOMBINE
→ EVALUATE

The system must be capable of answering:

- What was extracted?
- Why was it extracted?
- What changed?
- What remained?
- What was rejected?
- What interaction occurred with another input?
- What architectural consequence emerged?

This creates a design genealogy rather than a collection of references.

---

15 — UNCERTAINTY AS A SYSTEM OBJECT

Uncertainty is not automatically a defect.

It may represent:

- incomplete evidence
- unresolved historical interpretation
- speculative technology
- incomplete testing
- competing explanations
- open design potential

The system therefore preserves uncertainty rather than silently eliminating
it.

A documented uncertainty is preferable to an unsupported certainty.

---

16 — REJECTION AS KNOWLEDGE

Rejected material is part of the system's memory.

A rejected idea may reveal:

- a failed assumption
- a design boundary
- a technical limitation
- a methodological weakness
- an alternative path
- an important contradiction

Where useful, rejected work should remain accessible through the research or
design archive.

The objective is not to present an artificially perfect development history.

Potential attack domains include:

- historical accuracy
- source quality
- interpretation
- transformation logic
- cultural positioning
- structural plausibility
- environmental assumptions
- numerical reasoning
- originality
- methodological validity
- presentation logic
- technological claims

The system should preserve significant criticisms and corresponding responses

25 — AI GOVERNANCE

AI may participate in MERIDIAN as:

- research assistant
- analytical instrument
- generative tool
- comparative tool
- visualization instrument
- documentation assistant
- critique engine
- organizational system

AI is not automatically an author of architectural decisions.

AI-generated material must not be mistaken for evidence.

AI-generated historical or technical claims require independent verification.

AI visualization must remain subordinate to established architectural intent.

When AI contributes materially to a research or design process, the nature of
that contribution should be documented where relevant.

26 — COGNITIVE CLAIM GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN may investigate:

- attention
- curiosity
- memory
- pattern recognition
- interpretation
- surprise
- uncertainty
- cognitive conflict

However, the system prohibits unsupported scientific claims.

The following distinction must remain explicit:

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE
≠
THEORETICAL INTERPRETATION
≠
DESIGN HYPOTHESIS
≠
SPECULATION

No cognitive or neuroscientific claim should be presented as established merely
because it sounds plausible.

27 — FORKABILITY

The ecosystem is designed for intellectual extension.

A future contributor may fork:

- a question
- a research method
- a historical investigation
- a transformation operator
- a design experiment
- a material investigation
- a light system
- a mechanical hypothesis
- a design grammar
- a theoretical argument

A fork should preserve provenance.

At minimum:

PARENT
VERSION
AUTHOR
DATE
MODIFICATION
NEW QUESTION
NEW RESULT

Forking does not imply ownership of the original work.

It creates a traceable relationship between intellectual descendants.

---

28 — CONTRIBUTION MODEL

Future contributions should be evaluated according to their relationship to
the system.

A contribution should ideally identify:

- what it modifies
- why it modifies it
- what evidence supports the modification
- what new knowledge it creates
- what previous material it depends upon
- what remains uncertain

The ecosystem should favor contribution quality over contribution volume.

---

29 — COMPLEXITY GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN intentionally contains multiple layers.

However:

DEPTH ≠ DENSITY
COMPLEXITY ≠ CONFUSION
MYSTERY ≠ OBSCURITY
TECHNICALITY ≠ INTELLIGENCE
DOCUMENTATION ≠ KNOWLEDGE

Every additional system component must justify its existence.

Before adding a new layer, ask:

1. What problem does it solve?
2. What does it connect?
3. What new capability does it create?
4. What maintenance burden does it introduce?
5. Could an existing component perform the same function?
6. Does it improve architectural understanding?

If the answer is weak, the component should not be added.

---

30 — NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

The following rules govern MERIDIAN development.

1. Do not redesign established architecture without explicit reason.
2. Do not convert historical references into visual collage.
3. Do not fabricate evidence.
4. Do not fabricate technical feasibility.
5. Do not confuse interpretation with fact.
6. Do not confuse hypothesis with evidence.
7. Do not hide important uncertainty.
8. Do not erase meaningful failure.
9. Do not add complexity without function.
10. Do not use academic terminology as decoration.
11. Do not allow technology to replace architectural reasoning.
12. Do not allow documentation to become detached from architecture.
13. Do not allow beauty to substitute for evidence.
14. Do not allow evidence to eliminate architectural imagination.
15. Prefer relationships over isolated objects.
16. Prefer operators over metaphors.
17. Prefer transformation over reproduction.
18. Prefer emergence over forced symbolism.
19. Preserve provenance.
20. Preserve version history.
21. Preserve criticism.
22. Preserve unresolved questions.
23. Keep architecture at the center.
24. Treat research and design as recursive.
25. Optimize for intellectual integrity rather than apparent sophistication.

31 — SYSTEM QUALITY TEST

A mature MERIDIAN system should eventually allow a qualified reader to answer:

Architectural

- What is the architecture?
- What are its governing systems?
- Why does it have its present form?

Historical

- What historical knowledge entered the project?
- What was extracted?
- What was transformed?
- What was rejected?

Methodological

- How was transformation performed?
- Which operations were used?
- Which decisions were consequential?

Evidential

- What supports each major claim?
- What remains uncertain?

Critical

- What are the strongest objections?
- How does the project respond?

Technical

- Which mechanisms are demonstrated?
- Which are plausible?
- Which remain speculative?

Documentary

- How did the project evolve?
- What changed?
- Why did it change?

Ecosystemic

- How can another researcher continue from here?
- What can be forked?
- What remains open?

If these questions cannot eventually be answered, the system is incomplete.

32 — MATURITY MODEL

MERIDIAN may be evaluated through progressive maturity states.

M0 — Conceptual

Core idea exists.

M1 — Structured

System architecture and documentation structure established.

M2 — Evidenced

Major claims connected to evidence.

M3 — Traceable

Research and design genealogy reconstructable.

M4 — Critically Tested

Major assumptions exposed to structured criticism.

M5 — Archival

Stable releases and historical states preserved.

M6 — Forkable

External researchers can meaningfully extend parts of the system.

M7 — Generative

The methodology can produce new architectural investigations without
requiring reproduction of the original project.

The target is not maximum complexity.

The target is meaningful maturity.

36 — CHANGE CONTROL

Changes to MERIDIAN should be evaluated according to their scope.

Minor

Typographical correction, metadata correction, formatting improvement.

Structural

Folder, document, identifier or schema modification.

Methodological

Change to research, evidence or transformation methodology.

Architectural

Change to an established architectural decision.

Systemic

Change affecting relationships among major ecosystem components.

Significant changes should be documented through version history or changelog
records.

---

37 — PRINCIPLE OF REVERSIBILITY

Where practical, major methodological or structural decisions should remain
reversible until sufficient evidence exists to make them permanent.

This is especially important during early development.

The system should distinguish:

PROVISIONAL
→ TESTED
→ VALIDATED
→ ESTABLISHED

Premature closure should be avoided.

---

38 — PRINCIPLE OF EMERGENCE

MERIDIAN does not require every relationship to be predetermined.

Some architectural relationships may emerge through interaction among:

- historical inputs
- design variables
- constraints
- material conditions
- spatial relationships
- technical experimentation
- research findings

When an unexpected but defensible relationship emerges, the system should
document it rather than retroactively pretending it was always intentional.

---

39 — PRINCIPLE OF INTELLECTUAL HONESTY

MERIDIAN's credibility depends less on claiming certainty than on accurately
representing the boundary between certainty and uncertainty.

Therefore:

KNOWN → DOCUMENT
INFERRED → LABEL
HYPOTHESIZED → TEST / FRAME
SPECULATED → IDENTIFY
REJECTED → PRESERVE WHEN RELEVANT
UNKNOWN → RECORD

The system should never manufacture coherence after the fact.

Coherence should emerge from traceable relationships.

MERIDIAN is not merely a building documented online.

It is an attempt to construct a durable relationship between:

ARCHITECTURE
RESEARCH
HISTORY
TRANSFORMATION
DESIGN
EVIDENCE
CRITIQUE
DOCUMENTATION
MEMORY
AND
FUTURE POSSIBILITY

Its central ambition is not to preserve a finished answer.

The architectural artifact is the root.

The design process is the transformation layer.

The paper is the public argument.

The Dark Forest preserves unresolved possibility.
