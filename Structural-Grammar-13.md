# 13 — STRUCTURAL GRAMMAR

This is one of the places where MERIDIAN can either become real architecture or remain a formal exercise.

The structure should not arrive at the end and explain why the form is possible.

It should help explain why the form became what it is.

---

## Start with the big question

**What is carrying what?**

Not technically yet.
Architecturally.

Where is the building heavy?
Where is it spanning?
Where is it concentrating load?
Where is it releasing it?
Where does the geometry want continuity?
Where does it want separation?

The answer may change the architecture.

---

## The centre

If the centre is genuinely the main architectural event, there may be a structural reason for it to behave differently.

A larger span.
A concentrated load.
A ring.
A central support condition.
A roof that gathers forces.
A column-free room.

None of these should be invented just to justify centrality.

But if the spatial ambition creates one of them naturally, structure becomes part of the project's DNA.

---

## The peripheral field

Multiplicity becomes much more convincing when the repeated elements have a reason to repeat.

A structural bay.
A span.
A module.
A load path.
A changing section.

Then repetition is no longer ornament.

It is an architectural consequence.

This is also where the Achaemenid and other historical references can become useful at a deeper level.

Not "use columns."

Ask instead:

**What did repetition and structural order allow architecture to do?**

That question is transferable.
The column itself may not be.

---

## The dome

The dome studies are probably one of the most dangerous and promising parts of the project.

Dangerous because a dome can become an obvious symbol very quickly.

Promising because a dome is simultaneously:
space + structure + section + light + scale + silhouette.

The stepped or striated condition therefore needs to be tested through the whole thing.

If the steps change only the exterior image, they are weak.

If they change:
- how the shell carries,
- how the section rises,
- how light enters,
- how the interior expands,
- how the scale is perceived,

then the transformation becomes architectural.

---

## Split and structure

The split deserves a structural reading too.

Before:
one continuous load path.

After:
what happens?

Does the structure separate?
Does it bridge?
Does a new central void appear?
Does a transfer condition emerge?
Does the split force a different grid?

This could reveal that the original formal operation was actually pointing toward a deeper architectural problem.

---

## Not solved yet

We do not yet have enough evidence to declare the final structural system.

That is fine.

At this stage the useful work is to build structural hypotheses alongside spatial and formal hypotheses, then let them collide.

The goal is not to make the structure look expressive.

The goal is to reach a point where changing the structural logic would genuinely change the architecture.
