# 19 — EVALUATION

The project needs resistance.

Without resistance, almost any beautiful form can survive.

Evaluation is where the architecture has to defend itself.

---

## First: experience

Walk through it.

Where does the building become compressed?

Where does it release?

Where does the scale suddenly change?

Where does the centre become legible?

Where does it remain hidden?

Where does light make you stop?

Where does the building become ordinary?

That last question matters.

A project can have one extraordinary image and twenty ordinary spaces.

---

## Second: form

Look only at the mass.

No colour.
No ornament.
No render atmosphere.

Can you still see the logic?

Can you trace:
split → aggregation,
layer → exposure,
centre → field?

If not, the formal language may still be superficial.

---

## Third: section

The section should explain things the plan cannot.

The dome.
The steps.
The depth.
The light.
The vertical hierarchy.

If the section becomes interesting only after the render is added, the architecture is probably not doing enough.

---

## Fourth: structure

Ask whether the structural system agrees with the spatial ambition.

Not whether an engineer could somehow make it stand.

Whether the way it stands contributes to why it is shaped this way.

---

## Fifth: history

Remove the names.

Saint Peter.
Saint Basil.
Ilkhanid.
Achaemenid.

Now look at the building.

Does the architecture still have its own identity?

If yes, the sources may have been transformed.

If the building collapses conceptually without those names, it may still be leaning on reference rather than architecture.

---

## The hardest test

Take away the explanation.

Show someone:
plan + section + massing model.

Ask them what the building is doing.

Their answer will reveal things the written narrative can hide.

---

## One more test

Ask of every major move:

**Would I still make this decision if nobody ever saw the final image?**

If yes, there is probably architecture behind it.

If no, investigate why.
