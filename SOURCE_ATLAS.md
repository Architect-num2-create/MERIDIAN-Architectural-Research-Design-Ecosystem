00 — PURPOSE

"SOURCE_ATLAS.md" defines the source architecture of MERIDIAN.

It establishes how the project's external knowledge environment is organized,
classified, related, evaluated, and connected to architectural inquiry.

This document does not function as:

- a bibliography
- a source dump
- a literature review
- a research log
- an evidence ledger
- a collection of quotations
- a list of websites
- a list of historical buildings

Instead, the Source Atlas answers a different question:

«What knowledge territories does MERIDIAN depend upon, what kinds of
sources inhabit those territories, what can each source legitimately
establish, and how do those sources connect to architectural reasoning?»

The Atlas is therefore an architectural map of the project's knowledge
environment.

02 — CORE FUNCTION

The Source Atlas performs six primary functions:

1. Source Mapping
2. Source Classification
3. Source Evaluation
4. Source Relationship Mapping
5. Knowledge-Coverage Control
6. Architectural Relevance Mapping

These functions prevent MERIDIAN from treating all references as equivalent.

A peer-reviewed historical study, a conservation report, an archival drawing,
a museum database, a technical engineering paper, and a contemporary
architectural essay do not carry the same epistemic weight.

The Atlas makes those differences explicit.

---

03 — SOURCE PRINCIPLE

MERIDIAN follows the principle:

«A source is valuable not because it is interesting, famous, or visually
relevant, but because its evidentiary capacity matches the question being
investigated.»

Therefore:

QUESTION
   ↓
KNOWLEDGE REQUIREMENT
   ↓
SOURCE TYPE
   ↓
SOURCE
   ↓
EVIDENCE CAPACITY
   ↓
ARCHITECTURAL RELEVANCE

A source should never be selected merely because it supports an attractive
design idea.

05 — SOURCE IDENTIFICATION

Sources should receive stable identifiers.

Primary format:

SRC-###

Examples:

SRC-001
SRC-002
SRC-003

Additional namespaces may later be introduced for specialized source families,
but "SRC-###" remains the canonical source identifier.

A source ID should remain stable across repository versions whenever possible.

If the source record changes, its identity should not unnecessarily change.

08 — SOURCE AUTHORITY

Authority should be assessed relative to the question.

MERIDIAN therefore does not use a simplistic universal ranking in which one
source type is always superior to another.

For example:

- a conservation report may be stronger than a theoretical article for material condition;
- an engineering paper may be stronger than a historical monograph for
  mechanical feasibility;
- a primary archival document may be stronger than a secondary interpretation
  for a specific historical fact;
- a scholarly theoretical text may be stronger than a technical document for
  conceptual framing.

The correct question is:

«Authority for what claim?»

09 — SOURCE CONFIDENCE

S-A — High Confidence
S-B — Strong / Appropriate
S-C — Conditional
S-D — Weak / Contextual
S-U — Unverified

These classifications describe the current confidence in the source's
research utility.

They do not automatically determine whether every statement within the
source is true.

11 — SOURCE LIMITS

Every important source should eventually have a documented limitation.

Questions include:

- What does the source establish?
- What does it not establish?
- What assumptions does it make?
- What methodology produced the information?
- Is the evidence direct or mediated?
- Is the interpretation contested?
- Is the source geographically or temporally limited?
- Does the source provide architectural information or merely historical
  context?
- Can the source legitimately support a design decision?

The absence of this analysis is itself a research risk.

19 — SOURCE GAPS

A source gap exists when an important architectural question lacks sufficient
appropriate evidence.

Examples of potential gaps:

GAP-001
Historical evidence insufficient for a specific interpretation.

GAP-002
Architectural geometry insufficiently documented.

GAP-003
Technical feasibility lacks engineering evidence.

GAP-004
Iranian architectural relationship remains theoretically underdeveloped.

GAP-005
Reader-response assumptions lack empirical research.

A gap should not automatically trigger a search.

First determine:

1. Is the question actually necessary?
2. What kind of evidence is missing?
3. What source type could resolve it?
4. What happens if the gap remains unresolved?

HIGH ARCHITECTURAL CONSEQUENCE
+
HIGH UNCERTAINTY
+
HIGH CLAIM DEPENDENCY

This prevents research from becoming an endless accumulation of interesting

21 — SOURCE DISCOVERY VS SOURCE ADOPTION

MERIDIAN distinguishes:

Discovery

A source is encountered.

Screening

Its relevance and authority are examined.

Verification

Its claims and provenance are checked.

Adoption

It becomes an accepted research source.

Citation

Specific evidence is used in an argument.

Transformation

A legitimate architectural consequence is derived.

These stages must not be collapsed.

24 — SOURCE IDENTITY VS EVIDENCE IDENTITY

A critical distinction:

SRC-###

identifies a source.

EVD-###

identifies a specific piece of evidence extracted from a source.

One source may therefore produce many evidence objects.

Example:

SRC-014
   │
   ├── EVD-021
   ├── EVD-022
   ├── EVD-023
   └── EVD-024

This prevents the common error of treating an entire book, article, or
website as a single undifferentiated "fact."

---

25 — SOURCE VS CLAIM

Similarly:

SOURCE
   ↓
EVIDENCE
   ↓
CLAIM

A source does not automatically become a claim.

A claim is an intellectual statement constructed from evidence.

Therefore:

SRC ≠ EVD ≠ CLM

This distinction is foundational to MERIDIAN's epistemic integrity.

---

26 — SOURCE VS INTERPRETATION

MERIDIAN also distinguishes:

DOCUMENTED
      ↓
INTERPRETED
      ↓
TRANSFORMED

A historical source may document a condition.

The author may interpret its architectural significance.

MERIDIAN may then transform that interpretation into a design operation.

These three layers must remain traceable.

---

27 — SOURCE CONFLICT

When sources disagree:

Do not automatically choose the source that supports MERIDIAN.

Instead record:

CONFLICT
   ↓
SOURCE A
   ↕
SOURCE B
   ↓
REASON FOR DIFFERENCE
   ↓
EVIDENCE QUALITY
   ↓
INTERPRETIVE CONSEQUENCE

Possible reasons include:

- different methodologies
- different dates
- different definitions
- incomplete evidence
- historiographical disagreement
- reconstruction uncertainty
- disciplinary perspective

Conflict may become a research question.

---

28 — NEGATIVE SOURCE VALUE

A source can be useful even when it does not support the current hypothesis.

Examples:

- disproving an assumption
- exposing an anachronism
- demonstrating that a mechanism is implausible
- challenging an interpretation
- revealing an alternative history

Therefore:

«Sources are not selected only for confirmation.»

Disconfirming evidence is valuable.

---

29 — SOURCE SELECTION BIAS CONTROL

Before adopting a source, ask:

Am I selecting this source because it is authoritative?

or

Am I selecting it because it agrees with my design?

The second condition requires additional scrutiny.

MERIDIAN should actively search for:

- contradictory evidence
- competing interpretations
- alternative explanations
- technical objections
- historiographical disagreement

---

30 — ARCHITECTURAL RELEVANCE TEST

A source may be historically important without being architecturally useful.

For every major source ask:

R1 — What architectural variable does it expose?

R2 — What can be extracted?

R3 — What cannot legitimately be extracted?

R4 — What transformation could follow?

R5 — What architectural consequence could result?

R6 — What uncertainty remains?

A source that produces no meaningful architectural consequence may still belong
in the historical background, but should not automatically enter the design
genealogy.

34 — RESEARCH TERRITORIES VS DESIGN DECISIONS

The existence of a source territory does not predetermine a design decision.

For example:

ACHAEMENID SOURCES
        ≠
FIVE COLUMNS

The research must establish whether and how the historical knowledge can
legitimately contribute to the five-column system.

Likewise:

ST BASIL SOURCES
        ≠
SIX PERIPHERAL DOMES

The relationship must be documented through transformation.

And:

ILKHANID SOURCES
        ≠
ORNAMENT

The research must investigate spatial, geometric, threshold, material,
structural and environmental possibilities before reducing the relationship
to surface treatment.

---

35 — RESEARCH BOUNDARIES

The Source Atlas does not authorize unsupported claims.

In particular, MERIDIAN must not assume:

- that a historical form had a symbolic meaning merely because it looks
  symbolic;
- that a number has historical significance merely because it appears in the
  design;
- that a historical mechanism existed because a contemporary mechanism could
  resemble it;
- that a visual similarity establishes historical influence;
- that a modern reconstruction is equivalent to original historical evidence;
- that a theoretical concept automatically validates a design decision.

Every such transition requires evidence or explicit labeling.

37 — CORROBORATION

Important claims should be corroborated where appropriate.

A robust chain may look like:

PRIMARY RECORD
      +
SCHOLARLY ANALYSIS
      +
ARCHITECTURAL DOCUMENTATION
      +
TECHNICAL EVIDENCE
      ↓
HIGHER CONFIDENCE

The required number and type of sources depends on the claim.

Not every statement requires a large source cluster.

---

38 — SOURCE ECONOMY

MERIDIAN should prefer:

high-value sources

over:

large numbers of sources.

A smaller set of carefully selected sources with clear evidentiary roles is
preferable to a large undifferentiated bibliography.

Research density should increase understanding, not merely document count.

---

39 — DISCOVERY SOURCES

Search engines, general encyclopedias, blogs, social media posts, image
collections and other low-authority materials may be useful during discovery.

Their role should be:

DISCOVERY
   ↓
SOURCE TRACING
   ↓
AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE

rather than:

DISCOVERY SOURCE
   ↓
FINAL EVIDENCE

unless the discovery source itself is demonstrably authoritative for the
specific claim.

---

40 — VISUAL SOURCE CONTROL

Images are especially vulnerable to misinterpretation.

Before using an image as evidence, establish where possible:

- provenance
- date
- photographer / institution
- object identity
- viewpoint
- reconstruction status
- manipulation
- publication context

A visually persuasive image is not automatically historical evidence.

8. What other sources support or challenge it?
9. What uncertainty remains?
10. What downstream research or design question does it affect?

If these questions cannot yet be answered, the source may remain a discovery
lead rather than a foundational source.

---

52 — OPERATING PRINCIPLE

The Source Atlas follows one central rule:

«Do not ask only whether a source is relevant to MERIDIAN. Ask what
relationship the source creates inside MERIDIAN.»

A strong source does more than provide information.

It may:

- constrain an assumption
- expose a variable
- contradict another source
- generate a question
- validate an interpretation
- invalidate a hypothesis
- enable a transformation
- reveal a research gap
- connect two previously separate domains

This relational value is central to the Atlas.

53 — FOUNDATIONAL STATE

At Version 1.0, the Atlas is intentionally pre-populated.

No historical claim, technical claim, numerical interpretation, or theoretical
conclusion should be inserted here until it has passed through the MERIDIAN
research process.

The architecture of the knowledge system is established first.

The evidence comes next.

This prevents the repository from becoming a structure built around
premature conclusions.
