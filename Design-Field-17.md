# 17 — DESIGN FIELD

This is where the notebook should become useful to the person actually designing the building.

A design field is not a list of parameters.

It is the small set of things you can touch and change and immediately feel the architecture responding.

---

## Change one thing.

Make the centre wider.

Do not change anything else.

Look at the result.

Then move the centre.

Then lower it.

Then open it.

Then make the surrounding masses tighter.

Then loosen them.

Now you are learning what the building wants.

## But the important variables may not be visible.

For example:

degree of arrival.

Or:

amount of uncertainty before reaching the centre.

Or:

how quickly the building reveals its scale.

Those are architectural variables even though they are not objects you can point at.

They emerge from several physical variables acting together.

---

## A useful habit

When a variant feels better, do not immediately accept it.

Ask:

**What exactly got better?**

If the answer is vague, make another variant.

If you can name the change, you have found something.

If you can reproduce the change elsewhere, you may have found a rule.

---

## The field should stay open.

The project should not become a machine where every input has a predictable output.

The designer needs room to make a strange move.

Then test it.

That is still design.
