# 22 — UNBUILT / UNREALIZED

There will always be ideas that have not earned a place in the building yet.

That does not make them useless.

It only means they have not survived contact with architecture.

---

## Keep three things separate.

### An idea

"I wonder what happens if the dome is opened further."

Nothing has been proven.

### A hypothesis

"If the dome opens further, the central space may become less monumental but receive deeper light."

Now there is something to test.

### A design decision

"The dome is opened to this degree because the section, light and spatial scale work better."

This belongs to the building.

Do not collapse these three into one another.

---

## Unbuilt ideas can be important.

Maybe the project once needed:

a second centre,
a much deeper threshold,
a different structural grid,
an asymmetric field,
a completely different roof,
a darker interior,
a less recognizable historical reference.

Some of these may never return.

Keep them anyway.

They show the roads the architecture could have taken.

---

## But do not turn the archive into a graveyard.

An unrealized idea should stay only if it contains something useful.

A new question.
A spatial possibility.
A structural insight.
A formal relationship.
A better way of understanding an old problem.

If it contains none of these, let it go.

---

## The useful question

**What does this idea allow the building to do that it cannot do now?**

That is enough reason to test it.

If the answer is only "it looks interesting," it belongs somewhere else.
