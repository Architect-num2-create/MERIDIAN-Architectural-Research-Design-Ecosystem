# 21 — REJECTED ARCHITECTURES

A rejected form should not disappear.

Sometimes the thing that was wrong in the building is exactly what teaches us what the building is trying to become.

---

## Keep the actual version.

Not the cleaned-up story.

What did it look like?
What felt right?
What felt wrong?
At what point did it stop being architecture?

A sketch that was abandoned after ten minutes can be more revealing than a polished option that survived for three weeks.

---

## There are different kinds of failure.

### Beautiful but empty

The image works.
The architecture does not.

Usually there is no strong spatial consequence behind the formal move.

### Correct idea, wrong building

The transformation makes sense.
But the resulting spaces are weak.

This is useful.
The idea may survive even though the form does not.

### Too historical

The source is visible before the new architecture is.

This usually means the transformation stopped too early.

### Too complicated

Every part has an explanation.
Nothing has hierarchy.

The building becomes difficult rather than deep.

### Too controlled

Everything follows the rule.

Nothing unexpected can happen.

The project becomes a diagram of itself.

### Too arbitrary

The opposite problem.

There are interesting moments, but no relationship holds them together.

---

## Keep what was almost right.

Sometimes a rejected option contains one thing the final version loses:

a better entrance,
a stronger section,
a more convincing proportion,
a better shadow,
a stranger but more alive centre.

Do not throw that away just because the overall option failed.

---

## The most valuable question

**What did this failure teach me about what MERIDIAN is not?**

That negative knowledge is part of the architecture.

It narrows the field without pretending that the final answer was obvious from the beginning.
