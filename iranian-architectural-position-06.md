MERIDIAN — Iranian Architectural Position

Dossier: Research / Investigation
ID: 06
Status: Foundation

---

MERIDIAN does not begin with the assumption that an Iranian architecture must look Iranian.

That assumption is precisely what needs to be questioned.

Iranian architectural identity has often been represented through recognizable historical forms, motifs, ornaments, monumental references, or the revival of selected periods. Such approaches can produce immediate recognition, but recognition and continuity are not necessarily the same thing. Contemporary Iranian architectural discourse has repeatedly confronted this tension between historical reference, modernity, authenticity, and transformation.

MERIDIAN therefore treats Iranian architectural position as a research problem, not a visual answer.

---

From identity to relationship

The project is interested in whether something can remain meaningfully connected to Iranian architectural history after its recognizable appearance has been substantially transformed.

This shifts the investigation from:

What looks Iranian?

toward:

What relationships within Iranian architecture might still matter when their original forms are no longer reproduced?

These relationships may eventually be found in spatial organization, geometry, hierarchy, sequence, proportion, centre and boundary, relationship to landscape, environmental response, construction, material, light, or other architectural conditions.

But none of these should be declared inherently Iranian in advance.

They have to be investigated.

---

Continuity without imitation

Continuity is not assumed to mean preservation of appearance.

A historical architectural principle may survive through change, but the nature and extent of that continuity must be demonstrated rather than claimed.

This distinction is particularly important because the history of modern Iranian architecture contains repeated attempts to negotiate between inherited architectural traditions and changing contemporary conditions. Recent scholarship describes this field through competing approaches including imitation, transmission, and transformation.

MERIDIAN takes transformation seriously because it allows continuity and difference to exist simultaneously.

But transformation is not automatically successful simply because something has been changed.

The relationship to the source still has to be understood.

---

Iranian does not mean timeless

MERIDIAN does not treat Iranian architecture as one continuous, homogeneous language.

Iranian architectural history contains different periods, cultures, political conditions, technologies, environments, religious contexts, and architectural practices.

Even the idea of historical continuity can be constructed differently depending on how periods are selected and connected. Recent historiographical research on Iranian architectural history has shown that narratives of continuity are themselves interpretive constructions rather than neutral descriptions.

Therefore:

continuity must be investigated, not assumed.

A connection between two historical moments is meaningful only when the relationship can be supported.

---

What MERIDIAN refuses

This position rejects several shortcuts:

Motif = identity

A recognizable motif may communicate a reference without carrying its deeper architectural logic.

Antiquity = authenticity

Older does not automatically mean more authentically Iranian.

Similarity = continuity

A visual resemblance does not establish a historical or architectural relationship.

Abstraction = transformation

Changing the appearance of a historical element does not, by itself, transform its underlying logic.

Combination = synthesis

Putting several Iranian references together does not necessarily produce a new architectural system.

---

What remains open

MERIDIAN does not yet claim to know what the final Iranian position will be.

It may emerge through:

- spatial relationships rather than motifs
- operations rather than images
- principles rather than typological copies
- transformed historical intelligence rather than preserved appearance
- relationships between architecture and environmental conditions
- relationships between geometry, movement, light, material, and perception
- or through something the present research has not yet identified

The possibility of finding something unexpected must remain open.

---

The position

For MERIDIAN, being connected to Iranian architectural history does not require the architecture to behave like a historical replica.

The project instead asks whether historical inheritance can become an active architectural material.

Not preserved.

Not disguised.

Not merely quoted.

Transformed.

The validity of that position will not be established by declaring it.

It must be tested through historical evidence, comparison, extraction, transformation, and architectural consequence.

That investigation begins here.
