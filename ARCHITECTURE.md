# MERIDIAN — Architecture

## 01 — Purpose

This document defines the structural architecture of the MERIDIAN project.

MERIDIAN is organized as an interconnected architectural research, design,
documentation, and knowledge ecosystem.

The repository is therefore structured not only around files, but around
relationships between research, design, evidence, transformation,
documentation, critique, archive, and future development.

---

# 02 — Core Architecture

MERIDIAN operates through the following conceptual sequence:

HISTORY
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
EXTRACTION
↓
TRANSFORMATION
↓
SPATIAL / FORMAL SYSTEM
↓
SYNTHESIS
↓
EMERGENCE
↓
ARCHITECTURAL LANGUAGE
↓
PHYSICAL ARTIFACT
↓
DOCUMENTATION
↓
ARCHIVE
↓
FUTURE READER

This sequence describes the intellectual metabolism of the project.

It is not intended to function as a rigid linear algorithm.

Feedback, iteration, rejection, reinterpretation, and emergence may occur
between stages.

---

# 03 — System Layers

MERIDIAN is composed of interconnected layers.

## Layer 0 — Architectural Artifact

The physical architectural project.

It is the primary spatial and formal proposition through which the
methodology becomes architecture.

---

## Layer 1 — Research

The investigation of historical sources, architectural knowledge,
cultural conditions, spatial systems, formal systems, precedents,
theoretical positions, and relevant interdisciplinary knowledge.

Research establishes the evidence field from which transformation can occur.

---

## Layer 2 — Design / Transformation

The transformation of extracted knowledge into architectural operations,
systems, experiments, decisions, and synthesis.

This layer investigates how information becomes architectural form and space.

---

## Layer 3 — Scholarly Argument

The formal academic and architectural articulation of the project.

This includes the research paper, theoretical argument, methodology,
findings, discussion, limitations, contribution, and references.

---

## Layer 4 — Dark Forest

An exploratory research environment containing alternative paths,
unresolved questions, rejected propositions, hidden relationships,
experiments, speculative interpretations, and future research opportunities.

The Dark Forest is intentionally open-ended.

---

## Layer 5 — MERIDIAN Core

The evolving ecosystem surrounding the project.

The Core provides the infrastructure through which future researchers,
architects, students, and collaborators may inspect, extend, reinterpret,
test, document, or fork aspects of the project.

---

---

# 07 — Evidence Architecture

Where appropriate, project records distinguish between:

- Established fact
- Scholarly consensus
- Scholarly interpretation
- Authorial interpretation
- Hypothesis
- Experiment
- Design proposition
- Rejected proposition
- Unresolved question

This distinction prevents historical evidence, interpretation, speculation,
and architectural invention from being incorrectly presented as equivalent.

---

# 08 — Traceability

Significant architectural decisions should remain traceable to their
intellectual origins where possible.

A design decision may therefore be documented through:

ORIGIN
→ SOURCE
→ EVIDENCE
→ INTERPRETATION
→ OPERATION
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ DECISION
→ RESULT

Traceability is not intended to eliminate intuition.

It exists to make the reasoning behind the work inspectable.

---

# 09 — Iteration

MERIDIAN is iterative rather than strictly linear.

Possible feedback relationships include:

Research ↔ Design

Design ↔ Research

Design ↔ Technical Investigation

Paper ↔ Research

Paper ↔ Design

Critique ↔ All Layers

Archive ↔ All Layers

Future Research ↔ Previous Versions

A later discovery may therefore modify the interpretation of an earlier
design decision.

---

# 10 — Versioning

Major project states should be versioned.

Example:

v0.1 — Foundation
v0.2 — Research Development
v0.3 — Method Development
v0.4 — Design Integration
v0.5 — Prototype
v0.9 — Pre-Release
v1.0 — Major Release

Versions should document meaningful changes rather than arbitrary
file modifications.

---

# 11 — Archive Principle

The archive preserves both successful and unsuccessful development.

Where useful, it may preserve:

- Previous versions
- Rejected designs
- Failed experiments
- Alternative interpretations
- Research notes
- Earlier diagrams
- Historical references
- Critical comments
- Peer review
- Decision records

The archive therefore records evolution rather than only presenting a
polished final state.

---

# 12 — Critical Layer

MERIDIAN contains an explicit critical dimension.

The project should remain capable of being:

- questioned
- challenged
- compared
- criticized
- peer-reviewed
- falsified where applicable
- reinterpreted
- extended

Criticism is treated as an input into development rather than as a threat
to the project.

---

# 13 — Human Contribution

MERIDIAN is authored by a human designer and researcher.

AI-assisted tools may be used during research, visualization,
documentation, analysis, or experimentation where appropriate.

AI output does not automatically constitute evidence.

Sources, interpretations, decisions, and architectural propositions remain
subject to human evaluation and documentation.

---

# 14 — Ecosystem Principle

The repository is designed to support future extensions without requiring
the original architectural artifact to be redesigned.

The physical project establishes the initial case.

The methodology, archive, research, and Core provide the infrastructure
for future investigation.

---

# 15 — Future Architecture

The repository may eventually support:

- public research records
- structured datasets
- methodological protocols
- derivative investigations
- experimental branches
- collaborative research
- forks
- independent interpretations
- scholarly publications
- archival releases

These capabilities will be introduced only when their intellectual and
architectural requirements have been established.

---

# 16 — Architectural Integrity

The repository must never become more important than the architecture.

Digital infrastructure exists to clarify, preserve, investigate, and
extend the architectural work.

It must not replace architectural thinking with technological spectacle.

The central sequence remains:

HISTORY
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ EMERGENCE
