# Unsolved Mysteries, Research Seeds

Written at the difficulty of an actual puzzle. No answers supplied — that is the organ's whole function.

### Mystery 1 — The Grader's Reason
A faculty member suggested the unfinished sheet be framed under glass permanently. Framing is normally reserved for finished, exemplary work. What, specifically, was being preserved — a result, or a process caught mid-motion? No one has asked the faculty member directly. This is answerable, and it hasn't been answered.

### Mystery 2 — The DNA Extraction Order
Saint Basil and Saint Peter were combined first, under real time pressure, and the ziggurat-dome idea emerged from that collision rather than being designed toward. Ilkhanid and Achaemenid were added afterward. Does a two-stage origin — collision, then addition — leave a structural seam in the finished object that a careful eye could still find: a place where the building is visibly older in its thinking than the rest of it?

### Mystery 3 — The Hidden Name's Half-Life
A private, mysterious identity is meant to live inside the invisible narrative without compromising public academic standards. Every hidden thing eventually becomes findable — that is what separates it from a secret. What is this hidden name supposed to *do* once it is found, ten or twenty years from now, by someone who was never told where to look?

### Research seeds
Smaller, tractable threads for future dossier work:

- A side-by-side comparison of the Aperture Core against real historical qanat and wind-catcher light-and-air strategies, to see how much the "cross-era plausibility" claim survives contact with existing precedent.
- An actual structural back-of-envelope check of the 185-column rhythm against Ilkhanid arch-span norms — not to correct the symbolism (see C1 in Organ II), but to know exactly how far from load-bearing reality the symbolic choice sits.
- A comparison of the two-professor pattern — one steering toward the easier path, one later suggesting the diagrammatic presentation — as two very different models of what studio mentorship is actually for.
