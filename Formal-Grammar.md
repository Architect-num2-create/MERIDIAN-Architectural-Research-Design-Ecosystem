# 12 — FORMAL GRAMMAR

The form of MERIDIAN should feel like it had to become this way.

Not inevitable in the sense of being predictable.
Inevitable in the sense that, once you understand the relationships, the form makes sense.

The project already contains a few strong behaviours:

**split / aggregate / layer / open / curve / expose / differentiate / converge**

The important thing is not to collect more operations.

It is to find out which ones actually belong to this architecture.

---

## Split

A split is not interesting because one object becomes two.

It becomes interesting when the split changes something:

light,
movement,
structure,
scale,
view,
hierarchy,
or the relationship between inside and outside.

The unresolved question from the early work is still important:

**What was the original mass unable to do that forced it to split?**

That answer may be more important than the split itself.

---

## Aggregation

Aggregation can easily become "many pieces arranged nicely."

That is not enough.

The pieces need a relationship.

Same family, different size.
Same geometry, different height.
Same structural logic, different role.
Same centre, different distance.
Same material, different depth.

Then the many become one architectural field rather than a pile of objects.

---

## Layering and stepping

The layered mass and stepped dome studies suggest that vertical transformation matters in MERIDIAN.

But a step should never exist just because steps look monumental.

A step can be:
- structural,
- proportional,
- climatic,
- luminous,
- spatial,
- or a way of changing the apparent scale of a large volume.

The strongest version may be where several of these happen at once.

---

## Curvature

Radial curvature is potentially important because it can connect the centre to the field around it.

But curvature has to be earned.

What is bending?

The wall?
The circulation?
The roof?
The structural grid?
The view?
The ground?

If nothing except the silhouette bends, it is probably an image operation.

---

## Formal continuity

A good MERIDIAN form should survive a change of scale.

The same logic should be recognisable:

in the whole building,
in the plan,
in the section,
in a dome,
in a wall,
and eventually in a detail.

Not as repetition.

As **family resemblance**.

That is where the architecture can become deep without becoming complicated.

---

## One important danger

The project has enough formal material to become seductive very quickly.

Cross.
Radial geometry.
Multiple volumes.
Stepped domes.
Striation.
Openings.
Historical references.

Any one of these can produce a beautiful image.

Together they can also produce a building with no hierarchy.

So the formal question is not:

**How much can we combine?**

It is:

**Which few relationships are strong enough to generate the rest?**

That is the point where a vocabulary becomes architecture.
