DARK FOREST

A place for things that are not finished yet.

Dark Forest is the part of MERIDIAN where an idea is allowed to be uncertain.

Not every useful thing begins as a fact.

Sometimes it begins as a strange detail in a plan.
Sometimes as a contradiction between two sources.
Sometimes as a failure in the design.
Sometimes as a question that is too stupid to ask in a normal research folder.
Sometimes as a pattern that keeps appearing but cannot yet be explained.

This is where those things go.

Dark Forest is not an archive of leftovers.

It is not a collection of random references.

It is not a second research folder.

And it is definitely not a place to make weak ideas look intelligent by giving them complicated names.

The basic rule is simple:

«If we do not understand something, we do not pretend that we do. We investigate it.»

---

What belongs here?

Things that can disturb, extend, question, or change the project.

For example:

- an unexplained spatial relationship
- a historical detail that does not fit the current interpretation
- two pieces of evidence that disagree
- a design decision whose real origin is unclear
- a structural idea that looks promising but has not been tested
- a pattern found across unrelated buildings
- an assumption that might be wrong
- an alternative explanation
- a failed experiment
- a question that could become research
- a connection to mathematics, physics, cognition, biology, community, computation, or another field
- an idea that is currently too strange to place anywhere else

The important part is not how impressive the material sounds.

The important part is whether it can do something.

---

What does not belong here?

Do not put something here just because it is interesting.

Do not collect references to make the folder look intelligent.

Do not write speculative claims as if they were facts.

Do not turn every observation into a theory.

Do not manufacture explanations for decisions that were never actually explained.

Do not keep dead material simply because it looks beautiful.

Do not use Dark Forest to hide weaknesses in Research or Design.

A weakness is more useful when it is visible.

---

The Forest has no obligation to give an answer.

Some questions will be answered.

Some will become research.

Some will change the design.

Some will connect to another branch.

Some will be disproved.

Some will remain unresolved for years.

That is fine.

An unresolved question with a clean record is more valuable than a confident answer built on nothing.

---

The basic movement

Material can move through the Forest.

A rough version looks like this:

observation → question → investigation → contradiction → branch → test → failure / survival → new question

But this is not a conveyor belt.

Things can move backwards.

A dead branch can come back.

A research result can reopen an old question.

A design failure can create a new research seed.

A completely unrelated field can suddenly explain something that architecture could not.

The Forest should allow this.

---

One rule above everything else

Never confuse uncertainty with emptiness.

If something is uncertain, record the uncertainty.

If something is weak, record why.

If something is promising, record what makes it promising.

If something is wrong, let it be wrong.

If something changes the project, keep the trail.

That trail is part of MERIDIAN.

---

The first question to ask

Before adding anything:

«What could this change if we understood it better?»

If the answer is nothing, it probably does not belong here.

If the answer is unclear, investigate that too.

---

Relationship to the rest of MERIDIAN

Dark Forest does not replace the other dossiers.

It feeds them.

Research can send unanswered questions here.

Design can send failures here.

The Paper can send contradictions here.

The Forest can send something back when it becomes strong enough.

The point is not to keep the dossiers perfectly separated.

The point is to let useful material move between them without losing where it came from.

---

Keep the trail.

Every important object in the Forest should eventually allow someone to answer:

- Where did this come from?
- What made us notice it?
- What do we actually know?
- What are we assuming?
- What could be wrong?
- What have we tried?
- What happened?
- What changed because of it?
- Where could it go next?

If we cannot answer these questions yet, that is not a reason to delete the object.

It is a reason to keep investigating it.

Nothing here has to be finished.
Everything here has to be real.
