# Cognitive Branch

Independent of architecture, this is what the project actually trains in whoever spends real time with it:

- The discipline of holding a symbolic claim and a structural claim in the same sentence without letting one quietly borrow the other's credibility — the exact discipline Organ II's Contradiction Map exists to enforce.
- The practice of treating an unfinished object as data rather than as a failure to hide — the instinct the Case Anatomy's central fact (Organ I) was built to protect.
- The habit of keeping a contradiction map instead of a values statement, because a project that lists its own tensions is harder to fool, and harder to fool with.

**Where this organ's tools actually come from.** These aren't invented fresh for MERIDIAN — they're borrowed directly from a separate personal curriculum already running elsewhere: a Case File, an Evidence Engine, a Hypothesis Lab, an Epistemic Ledger. MERIDIAN is one live case running through that machinery, not a parallel invention that happens to resemble it. The Contradiction Map is an Epistemic Ledger wearing architecture's clothes; the Red Team entries are that curriculum's adversarial-reasoning practice, aimed for once at a building instead of an argument; the Unsolved Mysteries are that system's puzzle taxonomy, applied to a case with real stakes instead of a training exercise. The two systems were always going to meet somewhere. This dossier is where they do.
