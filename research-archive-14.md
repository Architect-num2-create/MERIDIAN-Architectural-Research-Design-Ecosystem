# Byzantine / Renaissance — Saint Peter's Basilica

## What is historically established

Saint Peter's dome history is more contested than its silhouette suggests.
Bramante's original 1506 plan proposed a Greek-cross church with a central
dome and four smaller subsidiary domes at the diagonal axes — five domes in
total, not the finished building's composition. Michelangelo, who later took
over the project, left no known designs for subsidiary domes at all; his
contribution was the central dome and its double-shell structure, completed
after his death. The subsidiary domes actually built at the front of the
church are the work of Carlo Maderno's collaborator Giacomo della Porta. The
two subsidiary domes that would have stood at the rear were never built,
because Maderno's later nave extension converted the plan from a Greek cross
to a Latin cross and removed the space they would have occupied.

In short: the building's dome composition passed through at least three
different architects' hands, and the version that exists today is not the
version any single one of them originally drew.

## What MERIDIAN extracts

MERIDIAN does not extract Saint Peter's appearance. It extracts:

- the **double-shell dome structure** — an inner structural shell and an
  outer protective shell separated by an interstitial space — as a
  transferable operator: *layered enclosure*.
- **axial hierarchy and cruciform organization** as an ordering logic for
  how a building declares a center.
- **centralized vertical emphasis** — the dome as the single event a whole
  plan organizes itself around.

## Where MERIDIAN's own numbers diverge

MERIDIAN's dome system is organized as one dominant central dome and six
subordinate domes. Saint Peter's built history does not offer a clean
precedent for "six subordinate": Bramante planned four, della Porta built
two, and two were never built at all. The six-dome figure in MERIDIAN is
therefore a project design decision, not a historical count — most plausibly
read as a deliberate synthesis between Saint Peter's subsidiary-dome logic
and Saint Basil's peripheral multiplicity (see [Russian](02_RUSSIAN.md)),
rather than a citation of either source individually. That synthesis is
worth stating outright in the [Design Dossier](../../02_DESIGN/), since it
is itself a piece of the project's argument, not something to leave
implicit.
