00 — PURPOSE

This document defines the governance principles, epistemic rules, architectural
authority, documentation requirements, review mechanisms, versioning principles,
and extension rules governing the MERIDIAN project.

MERIDIAN is not governed as a conventional architecture project, a software
repository, or a static academic document.

It is governed as an evolving architectural research ecosystem in which:

- architecture
- research
- design process
- historical interpretation
- experimentation
- documentation
- critical review
- archival memory
- and future extension

remain connected while retaining their distinct roles.

The purpose of governance is therefore not to control complexity for its own
sake.

The purpose is to ensure that complexity remains:

traceable, intelligible, defensible, extensible, and architecturally meaningful.

---

01 — GOVERNING PRINCIPLE

The fundamental governing principle of MERIDIAN is:

«Preserve the architectural identity; expose the reasoning; test the claims;
document the uncertainty; permit the system to evolve without falsifying its
history.»

MERIDIAN must be allowed to develop.

But development must not erase provenance.

A later interpretation must not silently replace an earlier one.

A hypothesis must not silently become a fact.

A speculative mechanism must not silently become an engineering claim.

A design revision must not silently rewrite the original design history.

The system must preserve both:

what MERIDIAN became

and

how MERIDIAN became it.

---

02 — PROJECT AUTHORITY

MERIDIAN has three distinct forms of authority.

02.1 Authorial Authority

The original author retains authority over:

- authorship of the project
- original architectural intentions
- original design decisions
- attribution
- interpretation of personal design history
- permission for major reinterpretation of the original artifact

External contributors, researchers, critics, and AI systems may analyze,
criticize, reinterpret, extend, or fork MERIDIAN.

They may not retroactively claim authorship of the original project.

---

02.2 Evidentiary Authority

No author, contributor, AI system, or later interpretation has authority over
what the evidence actually supports.

Evidence constrains interpretation.

When evidence contradicts an attractive narrative, the narrative must yield.

The project therefore prioritizes:

evidence over rhetoric.

---

02.3 System Authority

The governance architecture determines how information enters, moves through,
changes, and exits the MERIDIAN ecosystem.

System rules exist to protect:

- traceability
- provenance
- distinction between knowledge states
- version integrity
- intellectual honesty
- architectural continuity
- future extensibility

---

03 — CORE NON-NEGOTIABLES

The following principles are mandatory throughout MERIDIAN.

G-01 — Architectural Centrality

Architecture remains the center of the ecosystem.

Research, theory, technology, documentation, visualization, cognitive studies,
and digital infrastructure must ultimately establish a meaningful relationship
to architecture.

A concept that produces no meaningful architectural consequence should be
treated cautiously.

---

G-02 — No Historical Fabrication

MERIDIAN must never invent:

- historical facts
- historical mechanisms
- undocumented influences
- architectural precedents
- numerical meanings
- cultural relationships
- citations
- quotations
- technical precedents

When evidence is unavailable, the absence must remain visible.

---

G-03 — Evidence / Interpretation Separation

The system must distinguish between:

what is observed

and

what is inferred from the observation.

Evidence does not automatically equal interpretation.

Interpretation does not automatically equal fact.

---

G-04 — Hypothesis Integrity

A hypothesis must remain a hypothesis until sufficient evidence or testing
justifies a different epistemic status.

Interesting ideas are not promoted merely because they fit the project narrative.

---

G-05 — Speculation Integrity

Speculative design is permitted.

Unmarked speculation is not.

A speculative architectural mechanism must be explicitly identified as such.

---

G-06 — Anti-Collage Principle

Historical references must not be assembled as visual souvenirs.

Every major historical input should pass, where applicable, through:

OBSERVATION
→ DECOMPOSITION
→ EXTRACTION
→ ABSTRACTION
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ SYNTHESIS
→ EMERGENCE

The objective is transformation of architectural knowledge rather than
accumulation of recognizable historical imagery.

---

G-07 — Traceability

Important architectural and intellectual decisions should have a traceable
genealogy whenever practical.

A decision may connect to:

SOURCE → EVIDENCE → INTERPRETATION → HYPOTHESIS → OPERATOR → TRANSFORMATION → EXPERIMENT → RESULT → DECISION

Not every design decision will have a scholarly source.

Authorial intuition is legitimate.

It must simply be represented honestly as authorial intuition rather than
retroactively disguised as historical necessity.

---

G-08 — Preservation of Uncertainty

Unknowns are legitimate project states.

MERIDIAN must preserve uncertainty when uncertainty is genuine.

The project must never manufacture certainty merely to create a cleaner narrative.

---

G-09 — Rejection Is Knowledge

Rejected:

- hypotheses
- transformations
- interpretations
- design alternatives
- technical approaches
- historical readings

may constitute valuable knowledge.

Important rejection records should therefore be preserved rather than erased.

---

G-10 — Criticism Is Data

A credible criticism that exposes a weakness is not a threat to MERIDIAN.

It is information.

The system should preserve substantive criticism and, where appropriate,
record the resulting response or modification.

---

04 — EPISTEMIC GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN uses explicit knowledge states.

The principal classification system is:

Code| Knowledge State| Meaning
F1| Established Fact| Directly supported, verifiable information
F2| Scholarly Consensus| Broadly supported interpretation in relevant scholarship
F3| Plausible Scholarly Interpretation| Defensible but interpretive scholarly reading
I1| Authorial Interpretation| Interpretation originating from the project author
H1| Working Hypothesis| Proposition requiring further investigation or testing
X1| Experimental Proposition| Deliberately experimental or speculative design proposition
U1| Unknown| Important unresolved question or unavailable knowledge
R1| Rejected| Previously considered and intentionally rejected
D1| Design Decision| Adopted architectural decision
O1| Operational Rule| A rule governing the project/system

These classifications must not be treated as decorative labels.

They describe the epistemic status of project knowledge.

---

05 — KNOWLEDGE TRANSITIONS

Knowledge may change status.

For example:

U1 → H1 → X1 → D1

may occur during design development.

However, a transition must not be implied simply because an idea has been
repeated many times.

A status transition requires a documented reason.

Example:

«H1 → D1 because the proposition produced a successful spatial experiment
under the defined design constraints.»

Or:

«H1 → R1 because new evidence contradicted the hypothesis.»

The system should preserve the previous state rather than overwrite it.

---

06 — HISTORICAL SOURCE GOVERNANCE

Historical sources are not treated equally.

Sources must be evaluated according to:

- authority
- proximity to the subject
- methodological transparency
- scholarly context
- publication quality
- date
- primary / secondary status
- relevance
- limitations
- potential bias

A source may be useful without being authoritative.

A famous source may still be insufficient for a particular claim.

A secondary source may be valuable for orientation but insufficient for a
specific historical assertion.

---

07 — SOURCE CLAIM DISCIPLINE

For significant sources, MERIDIAN should ask:

1. What exactly does this source establish?
2. What does it not establish?
3. Which architectural variable does it expose?
4. Is the information direct evidence or interpretation?
5. What limitations does the source have?
6. Does another credible source disagree?
7. What architectural consequence, if any, can legitimately be derived?

The system must avoid the following chain:

SOURCE → UNJUSTIFIED CLAIM → DESIGN

The preferred chain is:

SOURCE → EVIDENCE → INTERPRETATION → TESTABLE / TRACEABLE CONSEQUENCE

---

08 — ARCHITECTURAL REFERENCE GOVERNANCE

Historical references entering MERIDIAN should be decomposed into architectural
domains where relevant:

- culture
- form
- space
- structure
- geometry
- proportion
- material
- light
- ornament
- symbolism
- context
- construction
- environment
- social logic
- ceremonial logic

The purpose is to prevent architectural references from being reduced to
surface appearance.

A reference becomes useful to MERIDIAN when its underlying architectural logic
can be examined.

---

09 — TRANSFORMATION GOVERNANCE

A transformation should identify, where practical:

INPUT

What was observed or extracted?

ABSTRACTION

What underlying principle was identified?

OPERATOR

What operation changes it?

CONSTRAINT

What limits the transformation?

OUTPUT

What architectural condition results?

RELATION

How does the transformed element interact with other systems?

TEST

How can the result be evaluated?

A transformation should not be justified solely by visual resemblance.

---

10 — THE ANTI-COLLAGE TEST

Before accepting a historical-derived architectural element, ask:

Question 1

Could the element be recognized merely because it visually resembles its
historical source?

If yes, investigate further.

Question 2

Can the project explain what architectural logic was extracted?

If no, the element is at risk of becoming quotation.

Question 3

Can the project explain what transformation occurred?

If no, the element is at risk of becoming reproduction.

Question 4

Does the transformed element interact with the wider MERIDIAN system?

If no, the element may be decorative attachment.

Question 5

Could the architectural consequence exist without the historical image being
directly visible?

If yes, the transformation is potentially deeper than visual imitation.

This test is not an absolute mathematical criterion.

It is a governance instrument for protecting the project's central thesis.

---

11 — ARCHITECTURAL BASELINE

The established architecture of MERIDIAN constitutes a protected design baseline.

This includes, among other established project conditions:

- seven principal domes
- one dominant central dome
- six subordinate peripheral domes
- zigguratic stepped dome concept
- courtyard system
- two interacting / converging architectural forces
- approximately 185 courtyard columns
- small arch rhythm
- approximately five principal Achaemenid-derived columns
- deep Iranian / Ilkhanid entrance logic
- historical transformation rather than direct reproduction
- light / shadow presentation system
- layered physical presentation
- research-linked architectural artifact

These conditions should not be casually redesigned.

A later contributor may propose an alternative.

However, an alternative must be represented as:

proposal / experiment / branch / fork

rather than silently replacing the baseline.

---

12 — RECONSIDERATION OF ESTABLISHED DESIGN

An established design decision may be reconsidered when:

- new evidence materially changes its basis
- structural analysis exposes a critical problem
- environmental analysis reveals a major contradiction
- spatial testing demonstrates failure
- a fundamental methodological contradiction is discovered
- the author explicitly chooses to reopen the decision
- a clearly documented experimental branch is created

Reconsideration should preserve the previous state.

The purpose is not to freeze MERIDIAN.

The purpose is to prevent historical revisionism within the project's own design
history.

---

13 — NUMERICAL GOVERNANCE

Numbers within MERIDIAN must not automatically receive symbolic meaning.

Examples include:

- 7 domes
- 5 major columns
- approximately 185 courtyard columns
- 3 dome levels
- 4 LEDs
- 5 cm frame depth
- 100 × 70 cm presentation sheet

For each meaningful numerical relationship, the system should distinguish:

- structural necessity
- geometric necessity
- proportional relationship
- historical precedent
- operational requirement
- material constraint
- presentation constraint
- authorial choice
- symbolic interpretation

A number must not be declared historically or symbolically meaningful without
evidence.

---

14 — TECHNICAL AND ENGINEERING GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN may contain speculative mechanisms.

However, technical language must be classified carefully.

The project must distinguish between:

Established Technology

A documented and demonstrable technology.

Technical Precedent

A documented system relevant to the proposed mechanism.

Engineering Hypothesis

A technically reasoned proposition requiring further validation.

Speculative Mechanism

A conceptual mechanism whose feasibility has not been established.

Design Proposition

A deliberately architectural proposal independent of proven feasibility.

No speculative mechanism may be described as technically proven unless it has
been appropriately validated.

---

15 — LIGHT / MECHANISM GOVERNANCE

The proposed moving dome / light relationship must therefore be treated with
epistemic discipline.

The project may investigate:

GEOMETRY → MECHANISM → MOVEMENT → LIGHT → TIME → EXPERIENCE

But the existence of the conceptual chain does not constitute proof of
engineering feasibility.

Future technical investigation should determine:

- load
- movement
- actuation
- structural stability
- maintenance
- weather exposure
- solar response
- control logic
- failure modes
- safety
- material behavior
- energy requirements

Until then, the mechanism remains a design proposition or hypothesis according
to its documented status.

---

16 — AI GOVERNANCE

AI may assist MERIDIAN in:

- research discovery
- information organization
- comparative analysis
- language assistance
- visualization
- image generation
- diagram development
- coding
- metadata generation
- archival organization
- hypothesis generation
- critical review

AI does not automatically become an author of MERIDIAN.

AI-generated output must not be treated as evidence merely because an AI system
produced it.

AI-generated historical claims require independent verification.

AI-generated architectural proposals must remain subordinate to the project's
architectural baseline and design logic.

AI may generate possibilities.

It does not independently establish:

- historical truth
- architectural authorship
- technical feasibility
- scholarly consensus
- cultural legitimacy

---

17 — AI HALLUCINATION CONTROL

When AI is used for research, the following rules apply:

1. Never cite a source that has not been verified.
2. Never invent page numbers.
3. Never invent quotations.
4. Never infer historical fact from visual similarity alone.
5. Never convert generated text into evidence without verification.
6. Record uncertainty when verification is incomplete.
7. Prefer primary or authoritative sources where appropriate.
8. Preserve the distinction between generated hypothesis and researched fact.

AI-assisted research is an input to the research system, not a replacement for
source criticism.

18 — RESEARCH GOVERNANCE

Research within MERIDIAN must follow the established "RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md".

The Research Ledger is the primary traceability mechanism.

Important research should ideally preserve relationships among:

SOURCE
→ EVIDENCE
→ CLAIM
→ INTERPRETATION
→ QUESTION
→ HYPOTHESIS
→ DECISION
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ EXPERIMENT
→ RESULT

Research should not be organized merely around the accumulation of documents.

Its purpose is to produce architectural intelligence.

---

19 — DESIGN GOVERNANCE

Design development should make visible, where practical:

- problem
- variable
- relationship
- decision
- test
- result
- revision
- rejection
- emergence

A design decision may originate from:

- evidence
- interpretation
- hypothesis
- spatial intuition
- technical constraint
- aesthetic judgment
- environmental reasoning
- authorial preference

These origins should not be artificially homogenized.

The goal is not to pretend every design decision was scientifically derived.

The goal is to make the actual origin of the decision legible.

---

20 — INTUITION GOVERNANCE

Architectural intuition is legitimate.

MERIDIAN does not require every design decision to originate from formal
research.

However, intuition must not be retroactively presented as evidence.

Where a design decision originates primarily from intuition, it may be recorded
as authorial intuition or design judgment and subsequently investigated.

This preserves the productive tension between:

INTUITION ↔ SYSTEM

rather than falsely eliminating intuition from the design process.

---

21 — RESEARCH / DESIGN RECURSION

Research and design are not independent pipelines.

The preferred relationship is:

RESEARCH ↔ DESIGN

Research may modify design.

Design may generate research questions.

Experiments may expose historical questions.

Historical analysis may invalidate design assumptions.

The governance system must therefore allow backward and forward movement.

---

22 — PAPER GOVERNANCE

The scholarly paper must remain academically defensible.

It must not:

- inflate weak evidence
- hide uncertainty
- present speculation as finding
- use terminology merely for prestige
- manufacture novelty
- exaggerate scientific conclusions

The paper should distinguish clearly among:

- established findings
- interpretations
- design propositions
- limitations
- future research

The paper is one representation of MERIDIAN, not the entire project.

---

23 — DARK FOREST GOVERNANCE

The Dark Forest is intentionally more permissive than the formal research layer.

It may contain:

- speculative hypotheses
- unresolved questions
- alternative readings
- contradictions
- strange but defensible ideas
- failed paths
- future research seeds

However, speculative content must remain visibly distinguishable from
established knowledge.

The Dark Forest is a space for intellectual exploration, not a license for
fabrication.

---

24 — CORE GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN CORE defines the extensibility of the ecosystem.

Future extensions may modify:

- methods
- research questions
- transformation operators
- experiments
- documentation structures
- design grammars
- research branches

Extensions must preserve provenance.

A derivative work should identify:

- parent project
- parent version
- author
- modification
- reason for modification
- new research
- new result

---

25 — FORK GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN is intended to be forkable.

A fork may reproduce:

- a question
- a research method
- a transformation operator
- a design experiment
- a design grammar
- a research branch

A fork does not automatically reproduce the original authorship.

Forks should preserve:

ORIGIN → PARENT → MODIFICATION → NEW AUTHOR → NEW RESULT

A fork may disagree fundamentally with the parent.

Disagreement is permitted.

Loss of provenance is not.

---

26 — CONTRIBUTOR GOVERNANCE

Contributors may participate through:

- research
- evidence
- criticism
- interpretation
- hypothesis
- experiment
- design extension
- technical analysis
- documentation
- archival work

Contributors should identify the nature and epistemic status of their
contribution.

Contributions that challenge MERIDIAN are welcome when they are reasoned and
traceable.

Agreement is not a prerequisite for contribution.

---

27 — PEER REVIEW

MERIDIAN should maintain a culture of internal adversarial review.

Reviewers may challenge:

- historical claims
- source selection
- interpretation
- transformation logic
- structural assumptions
- technical claims
- numerical reasoning
- Iranian architectural positioning
- originality claims
- cognitive claims
- graphical communication
- methodological claims

A criticism should ideally identify:

CLAIM → PROBLEM → EVIDENCE → CONSEQUENCE → RECOMMENDATION

Reviews should be preserved when they materially influence the project.

---

28 — CONTRADICTION PRESERVATION

Contradictions should not automatically be resolved.

Where credible evidence conflicts, MERIDIAN may preserve both positions while
recording:

- source A
- source B
- nature of disagreement
- confidence
- current project interpretation
- unresolved implications

A clean narrative is not always a truthful narrative.

---

29 — ERROR CORRECTION

When an error is discovered:

1. Do not silently rewrite history where the previous state is meaningful.
2. Record the correction.
3. Identify the affected claim or document.
4. Explain the reason for correction.
5. Update dependent material where necessary.
6. Preserve the previous version in repository history or archive.

The objective is:

correction without historical erasure.

---

30 — VERSION GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN uses versioning to represent meaningful states of development.

Versions should distinguish between:

- working states
- experimental states
- reviewed states
- released states
- archived states

A version increment should correspond to meaningful change rather than
arbitrary activity.

The project should preserve the relationship between:

VERSION → CHANGE → REASON → CONSEQUENCE

---

31 — ARCHIVE GOVERNANCE

Archived material should remain available when it has historical, research, or
design significance.

Archiving does not mean endorsement.

An archived hypothesis may be wrong.

An archived design may be unsuccessful.

An archived interpretation may later be disproven.

The archive exists to preserve the genealogy of thought.

---

32 — PUBLIC / INTERNAL DISTINCTION

MERIDIAN may contain different levels of visibility.

Potential categories include:

PUBLIC

Suitable for general access.

RESEARCH

Public but primarily intended for scholarly or architectural readers.

EXPERIMENTAL

Unresolved or speculative material.

ARCHIVAL

Preserved historical material.

PRIVATE

Material intentionally withheld by the author.

Privacy must never be used to conceal fabricated evidence or materially
misrepresent the public research argument.

---

33 — HIDDEN LAYER GOVERNANCE

A future hidden layer may contain:

- secondary pathways
- cross-references
- subtle relationships
- encoded navigation
- deeper interpretive structures

However:

The hidden layer must never:

- fabricate evidence
- falsify history
- manipulate scholarly conclusions
- deceive readers about project status
- undermine accessibility of the public artifact

The hidden layer is optional intellectual depth, not epistemic authority.

---

34 — READER GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN is designed for multiple depths of engagement.

Readers may enter through:

- architecture
- image
- history
- research
- design
- criticism
- paper
- Dark Forest
- Core

However, deeper claims should require deeper evidence.

The project should support progressive discovery without deliberately making
basic information inaccessible.

EVIDENCE

INTERPRETATION

40 — METADATA INTEGRITY

Metadata must describe the document truthfully.

Do not mark a document:

FINAL

when it is experimental.

Do not mark a hypothesis:

FACT

because it has been repeated.

Do not mark a draft:

RELEASED

because it looks complete.

Status is epistemic and operational information, not decoration.

---

41 — VISUALIZATION GOVERNANCE

Visualizations, renders, diagrams, and AI-generated images must not be treated
as historical evidence unless independently supported.

A visual may communicate:

- a design proposition
- a reconstruction
- a diagram
- an experiment
- a speculative scenario

Its role must be identified.

Visual persuasion must never substitute for evidence.

---

42 — PRESENTATION GOVERNANCE

The physical 100 × 70 cm artifact, frame, LEDs, shadows, hidden diagrams, and
surface treatments are part of MERIDIAN's architectural argument.

However, presentation effects must remain connected to the conceptual system.

The physical presentation should reinforce:

CHANGE → VISIBILITY → INVISIBILITY → EMERGENCE

rather than become independent theatrical decoration.

---

43 — AESTHETIC GOVERNANCE

MERIDIAN seeks integration of:

ART + ARCHITECTURE + KNOWLEDGE

Beauty is legitimate.

Beauty is not evidence.

Complexity is legitimate.

Complexity is not intelligence.

Mystery is legitimate.

Mystery is not proof.

Technology is legitimate.

Technology is not sophistication by itself.

---

44 — ANTI-PRESTIGE RULE

MERIDIAN must not use:

- unnecessary academic jargon
- fashionable theoretical terminology
- neuroscience terminology
- systems terminology
- AI terminology
- mathematical language

merely to make the project appear more sophisticated.

Every specialized concept should perform actual analytical work.

---

45 — DECISION QUALITY

When making a major project decision, MERIDIAN should ask:

1. What problem is being solved?
2. What evidence exists?
3. What assumptions are being made?
4. What alternatives exist?
5. Why is this option preferred?
6. What are the consequences?
7. What could falsify or weaken the decision?
8. What remains unknown?
9. How does it affect the architecture?
10. How should the decision be documented?

---

46 — SYSTEM COMPLEXITY RULE

MERIDIAN should pursue:

meaningful complexity

rather than:

unnecessary complexity.

A new layer should justify its existence by providing at least one meaningful
function such as:

- increased traceability
- increased research quality
- increased design clarity
- increased critical capacity
- increased archival value
- increased extensibility
- increased reader value

If a layer provides none of these, it should be reconsidered.

---

47 — PUBLIC CLARITY RULE

Internal complexity must not destroy public clarity.

A reader should be able to understand:

- what MERIDIAN is
- what the architecture is
- why it exists
- where the research is
- where the design process is
- where the paper is
- where unresolved questions are
- where the archive is

without understanding the entire internal system.

---

48 — SYSTEM EVOLUTION

Governance itself may evolve.

If a governance rule becomes inadequate, it may be modified through a documented
revision.

A governance change should record:

OLD RULE → PROBLEM → PROPOSED CHANGE → REASON → NEW RULE → VERSION

Governance must therefore remain stable enough to preserve identity but flexible
enough to support learning.

---

49 — GOVERNANCE PRIORITY ORDER

When two project principles appear to conflict, use the following priority
order:

1. Truthfulness

Do not fabricate.

2. Provenance

Preserve where information came from.

3. Architectural Integrity

Protect the identity and logic of the project.

4. Epistemic Clarity

Distinguish fact, interpretation, hypothesis, experiment, and speculation.

5. Traceability

Make important decisions reconstructable.

6. Critical Openness

Allow credible criticism and revision.

7. Extensibility

Permit future research and forks.

8. Aesthetic Ambition

Preserve beauty and artistic power.

9. Complexity

Add complexity only when it produces meaningful value.

This ordering is not intended to diminish artistic ambition.

It exists to prevent aesthetic ambition from overriding intellectual integrity.

---

50 — GOVERNANCE FAILURE CONDITIONS

The MERIDIAN governance system is considered to be failing if:

- historical claims cannot be traced
- hypotheses become indistinguishable from facts
- design decisions lose provenance
- rejected ideas disappear without explanation
- AI-generated claims enter the research layer unverified
- speculative engineering is represented as proven
- historical references become decorative collage
- documentation becomes disconnected from architecture
- complexity becomes an end in itself
- the repository becomes impossible to navigate
- the project cannot explain why major decisions were made

If these conditions occur, governance must be reviewed before further expansion.

---

51 — MINIMUM INTEGRITY TEST

Before a major component enters a released MERIDIAN state, ask:

Can we explain what it is?

Can we explain why it exists?

Can we identify its origin?

Can we distinguish evidence from interpretation?

Can we identify uncertainty?

Can we explain its architectural consequence?

Can we identify what could be wrong?

Can another researcher follow its genealogy?

If the answer is substantially no, the component should remain:

draft / experimental / unresolved

until its status becomes clearer.

---

52 — FINAL GOVERNANCE POSITION

MERIDIAN is not governed by the demand that everything be certain.

It is governed by the demand that uncertainty be honest.

It is not governed by the demand that every design decision be scientifically
derived.

It is governed by the demand that the origin of decisions not be falsified.

It is not governed by the demand that every historical reference disappear.

It is governed by the demand that references become architectural knowledge
rather than visual souvenirs.

It is not governed by the demand that the project stop changing.

It is governed by the demand that change remain traceable.

The fundamental governance loop is therefore:

OBSERVE
→ RECORD
→ CLASSIFY
→ QUESTION
→ TEST
→ DECIDE
→ DOCUMENT
→ REVIEW
→ REVISE
→ ARCHIVE

And the deepest governing principle remains:

«MERIDIAN must be allowed to evolve without losing the memory of what it was,
the evidence for what it claims, or the architectural intelligence that made
it worth evolving.»
