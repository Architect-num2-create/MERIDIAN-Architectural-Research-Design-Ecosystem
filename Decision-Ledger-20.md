# 20 — DECISION LEDGER

This is not project management.

It is the memory of the architect.

A building is full of decisions that disappear once the final form is fixed.

That is dangerous.

Because later you can forget why something was there and start defending it simply because it already exists.

---

## Keep the moment.

**Decision:**  
What did I actually choose?

**Because:**  
What was I responding to?

**Alternative:**  
What else could I have done?

**Consequence:**  
What changed in the architecture?

**Status:**  
Still alive / accepted / temporary / rejected

---

## The important part is the alternative.

Without the alternative, a decision starts looking inevitable.

It usually wasn't.

For example:

**The centre is separated from the peripheral masses.**

Possible reason:
the central volume needed its own scale and light.

But perhaps another option was:

**The centre is embedded inside the field.**

If both were plausible, the choice matters.

Now we know there was actual design happening.

---

## Record uncertainty too.

"I don't know yet why this works."

is a valuable architectural note.

So is:

"I made this intuitively. The reason came later."

And:

"I can explain it, but I am not sure the explanation caused the decision."

These are not weaknesses.

They are part of how design actually happens.

---

## Never rewrite history.

If a later theory makes an old decision sound brilliant, do not silently change the original reason.

Keep both:

what you thought then,

and what you understand now.

That difference may become one of the most valuable parts of the project.

---

## The ledger should eventually reveal the architect.

Not because it says who designed the building.

Because it shows **how the building was actually thought into existence.**
