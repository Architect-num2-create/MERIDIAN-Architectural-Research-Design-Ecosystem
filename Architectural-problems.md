# MERIDIAN — Architectural Problems

MERIDIAN is not interesting because it has many references.

It becomes interesting if it can solve a few difficult architectural problems well.

### How do you transform history without making a collage?

This is the first problem.

The project started from Saint Peter and Saint Basil and later opened toward Iranian references. The danger is obvious:

take a plan from here, domes from there, entrance from somewhere else, ornament from another place.

That produces a building with references.

Not necessarily an architecture.

The real problem is to find a relationship between unlike architectural logics and make that relationship generate something neither source contained.

### How does multiplicity remain architecture?

Many parts are easy.

Making many parts belong to one architectural order is hard.

The peripheral elements need difference, but they also need relationship.

Too similar → repetition.

Too different → collection.

The centre must therefore do more than sit in the middle. It must establish a field.

### Why should the building be split?

This is still unresolved.

The split is visibly important in the process, but its architectural necessity has not yet been proven.

It could be:

a response to scale,

a way of creating courtyards,

a way of producing movement,

a structural break,

a spatial hierarchy,

a reaction to the site,

or simply an early formal intuition.

We should not invent the answer.

We need to find it.

### How does the dome become architecture rather than decoration?

A stepped circular dome is visually strong.

That is exactly why it is dangerous.

If it remains only a silhouette, it is decoration.

For it to become architecture, the stepping has to affect something real:

structure,

section,

light,

ventilation,

movement,

acoustics,

construction,

or spatial experience.

The existing project record already points toward light and a possible mechanical relationship, but this remains a hypothesis, not an established fact.

### How does Iranian identity enter without becoming costume?

The entrance, vault, geometry, proportion, depth, light and material are stronger places to begin than copying an Iranian façade.

The question is not:

"How can this look Iranian?"

It is:

"Which Iranian architectural intelligence can change the building itself?"

### How much complexity does the architecture actually need?

The project contains a lot.

But complexity is not automatically depth.

Some complexity can be structural.

Some spatial.

Some geometric.

Some atmospheric.

Some historical.

Some can simply be removed.

A mature MERIDIAN should be able to lose detail without losing its architecture.

### What is intuition doing?

This may be the hardest problem.

Some of the strongest decisions may have appeared before their explanations.

That is normal in architecture.

The task is not to pretend they were logical from the beginning.

The task is to discover which intuitions were genuinely architectural and then test them.

---

The unresolved parts should stay visible.

A solved-looking project can be intellectually weaker than a project that knows exactly where it still does not know.
