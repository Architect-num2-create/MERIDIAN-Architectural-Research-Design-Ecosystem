# MERIDIAN — Design Intent

I do not want to write this as a manifesto.

The intent is simpler.

I want to make a building that could not have been produced by simply choosing a historical style.

The historical buildings are starting points.

They contain knowledge about space, hierarchy, proportion, structure, threshold, repetition, light and form.

I want to take that knowledge apart.

Then change it.

Then see what survives.

The building should therefore feel historically informed without looking historically copied.

That distinction is important.

---

The centre matters.

But I do not yet want to decide that the centre matters for symbolic reasons.

Architecturally, it needs to prove itself through space, movement, structure, light and hierarchy.

The surrounding elements should not merely decorate the centre.

They should make the centre possible, challenge it, frame it, compress it, release it, or give it scale.

---

I want the building to contain difference without falling apart.

One mass can split.

The split can create new relationships.

The new parts can aggregate.

Aggregation can create a larger order.

The order can still allow difference.

That is more interesting to me than perfect symmetry.

---

I want the dome to stop being an inherited object.

If it is stepped, the steps need a reason.

If they affect light, they need to actually affect light.

If they move, the mechanism has to make architectural sense.

If they change the section, the section has to become spatially meaningful.

Otherwise the dome should stay simple.

---

I want Iranian architecture to enter through intelligence, not costume.

Depth instead of pasted ornament.

Proportion instead of imitation.

Threshold instead of façade styling.

Geometry that does something instead of geometry that merely fills a surface.

Structure that carries the memory instead of a capital placed on top of a column just to announce its origin.

---

And I want one rule to remain uncomfortable:

**the architecture is allowed to surprise me.**

If a transformation produces something I did not expect, I should not immediately force it back into the original idea.

That may be where emergence actually happens.

But emergence cannot be an excuse for randomness.

The final building still has to stand, move, gather people, control light, make space, carry structure and make architectural sense.

---

So the intent, for now, is not to make a historical hybrid.

It is to find out whether different architectural inheritances can enter one building, change one another, and leave behind a language that belongs to neither source completely.

And then make that language architectural.
