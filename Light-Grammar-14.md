# 14 — LIGHT GRAMMAR

Light should not decorate the architecture after the form is finished.

In a building with depth, thick mass, layered geometry, domes, courtyards and controlled openings, light can actually be one of the things that makes the transformations legible.

---

## Think in conditions, not effects.

Not:
"dramatic light."

Instead:

thin opening → narrow beam  
deep wall → slow transition  
high opening → distant light  
low opening → grazing light  
central opening → concentrated light  
layered roof → changing intensity  
courtyard → reflected / indirect light

These are architectural relationships.

---

## The centre

The centre does not automatically deserve the most light.

It might be the brightest place.
It might be the darkest.
It might receive light from above while the surrounding spaces receive side light.
It might change during the day.

The interesting question is:

**What should light make the centre feel like?**

A destination?
A void?
A sacred-scale volume?
A calm place?
A place where all the geometry suddenly becomes understandable?

---

## The edge

The perimeter can control how the outside world enters.

Small openings can make the wall feel deep.
Large openings can dissolve it.
Repeated openings can establish rhythm.
Directional openings can control views.

This connects directly to the existing "Directional Openings" study.

The next step is not to make prettier openings.

It is to discover what those directions are doing to space.

---

## Section matters more than elevation

A façade can tell us where an opening is.

A section tells us what the light actually does.

That makes the section especially important for MERIDIAN.

The layered vertical mass, the stepped dome and the sectional exposure studies should eventually be tested together.

A small change in height can change:
the angle of light,
the depth of shadow,
the apparent thickness,
the scale of the room,
and the way the roof is perceived.

That is much richer than adding a pattern.

---

## Time

The building should not have only one lighting condition.

Morning, noon, afternoon and artificial night light can reveal different relationships.

This is particularly important if radial and layered geometries are involved.

A geometry that looks powerful at noon but becomes dead for most of the day is not necessarily successful.

---

## A useful test

Render the same space without materials.

Then without colour.

Then without artificial light.

Then only as a section.

If the spatial logic still has a strong relationship with light, the architecture is doing the work.

If the effect disappears completely, the light has probably been used as an image.
