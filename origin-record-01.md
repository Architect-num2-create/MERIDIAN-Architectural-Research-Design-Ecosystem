MERIDIAN — Origin Record

Dossier: Research / Investigation
ID: 01
Status: Foundation

---

MERIDIAN did not begin as a research system.

It began as a design problem.

The initial task was to work with a historical architectural subject and produce a highly detailed architectural presentation. The expected direction could easily have been reproduction: study a historical building, borrow its visible language, and construct a convincing contemporary representation of it.

That was not the direction taken.

The first important decision was a refusal:

I could not simply copy history.

The question that followed was more productive:

If the historical source cannot be reproduced, what can actually be taken from it?

This shifted the project from imitation toward transformation.

At this stage, the distinction was not yet a complete methodology. It was an architectural intuition.

Historical architecture began to be treated less as an image and more as something that could be opened, examined, separated into parts, and understood through the relationships operating inside it.

That led gradually toward a different sequence:

"History → Observation → Decomposition → Extraction → Transformation"

The project then began to ask what might happen after transformation.

Not:

«How can this historical form be redrawn?»

But:

«What architectural condition could emerge if its underlying logic were changed?»

This became the point from which MERIDIAN developed.

---

The first tension

The early project was produced under ordinary studio conditions rather than inside a carefully prepared research programme.

There was limited experience, limited time, and pressure to produce a complex architectural result.

That condition mattered.

Some decisions came from analysis. Others came from intuition, drawing, experimentation, technical limitation, or simply the need to move the project forward.

The distinction between these things was not always clear at the beginning.

In retrospect, this became one of the most important reasons for documenting the project more carefully.

MERIDIAN should not rewrite its own history and pretend that every later concept existed from the beginning.

The development matters.

The uncertain steps matter.

The decisions that appeared first as intuition may later acquire an explanation. Others may remain unresolved.

Both belong to the record.

---

From form to operation

As the design developed, historical references began to be examined through architectural relationships rather than isolated motifs.

Geometry could be separated from appearance.

A column could be understood through its role in order and hierarchy rather than simply as a column.

A dome could be examined through centrality, layering, proportion, structure, sequence, or sectional behaviour rather than treated as a recognizable image.

Ornament could become a question of repetition, density, rhythm, surface, or perception.

The same historical source could therefore produce different architectural operations depending on what was extracted from it.

This was the beginning of the project's transformation logic.

The important distinction became:

"source ≠ extraction ≠ transformation ≠ result"

A transformed element was no longer required to resemble its source.

---

When the project became larger than the original design

The physical architecture eventually exposed questions that could not be answered by the design alone.

Where did a transformation actually come from?

Was it historically supported, inferred, invented, or simply useful?

Why was one reading accepted and another rejected?

When does a historical reference stop being a reference and become a new architectural operation?

What remains of history after transformation?

These questions changed the nature of the project.

The need for a research layer was not added as decoration around an already finished design.

It emerged because the design itself was generating questions.

From there, MERIDIAN began to develop as a research-and-design ecosystem rather than only a final architectural proposal.

---

What this record preserves

This document records the project as it developed, not as it can be explained after the fact.

Later frameworks, classifications, research systems, and theoretical language should not be projected backwards onto the earliest decisions unless there is evidence that they were actually present.

The origin is therefore not treated as a polished founding myth.

It is a starting condition.

The value of recording it is precisely that future development can be compared against it:

what changed,
what survived,
what was abandoned,
what was discovered later,
and what the project eventually became.

---

Origin principle

MERIDIAN began with a refusal to reproduce history.

Its research emerged from the attempt to understand what could remain after reproduction was refused.

"History → Transformation → Emergence"
