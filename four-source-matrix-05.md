MERIDIAN — Four-Source Matrix

Dossier: Research / Investigation
ID: 05
Status: Foundation

---

The four historical fields in MERIDIAN are not four styles waiting to be combined.

They are four different territories through which the project can test how architecture carries, organizes, and transforms spatial intelligence.

The matrix exists to prevent premature synthesis.

Before asking what can be combined, MERIDIAN must first understand what is actually different.

---

What the matrix compares

Each field should eventually be examined through the same set of architectural lenses where meaningful:

Dimension| Question
Geometry| How is geometric order constructed?
Spatial organization| How are spaces related to one another?
Hierarchy| Where does importance or dominance come from?
Axis / Centre| How are direction, centrality, or orientation produced?
Mass / Void| How do solid and empty space establish relationships?
Sequence| How does architecture unfold through movement?
Threshold| How is transition between conditions constructed?
Structure| How does structural logic affect architectural order?
Proportion| What relationships govern scale and measure?
Repetition / Rhythm| How are continuity and variation produced?
Light / Shadow| How do illumination and darkness participate in spatial experience?
Surface / Ornament| How are pattern, density, and visual information organized?
Material| What architectural role does material play beyond appearance?
Context| What historical, environmental, social, or cultural conditions shape the architecture?

Not every dimension will be equally relevant to every source.

An empty cell is preferable to an invented answer.

---

What the matrix is looking for

The matrix is not searching for similarities alone.

It should reveal four kinds of relationships:

Convergence
Different traditions produce related architectural conditions.

Difference
Similar architectural problems are resolved through substantially different systems.

Specificity
A characteristic depends strongly on the particular historical context from which it emerged.

Potential
A relationship appears capable of becoming an architectural operation after transformation.

The fourth category must remain provisional until it survives further investigation.

---

A rule against artificial synthesis

MERIDIAN does not assume that the four fields should eventually merge.

A combination is meaningful only when a defensible relationship exists between the things being combined.

Historical proximity is not enough.

Visual compatibility is not enough.

Aesthetic similarity is not enough.

If two sources resist combination, that resistance is itself useful knowledge.

The matrix must therefore preserve incompatibilities rather than smoothing them away.

---

What this matrix should eventually reveal

The goal is not to conclude that:

«all four traditions contain the same principles.»

Nor is it to prove that:

«each tradition is completely unique.»

The useful territory lies between these extremes.

The matrix should help MERIDIAN discover which architectural relationships recur, which remain historically specific, which contradict one another, and which can survive transformation into a new architectural condition.

Until that investigation is performed, the matrix remains intentionally incomplete.
