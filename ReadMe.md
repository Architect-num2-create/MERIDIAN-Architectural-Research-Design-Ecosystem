# MERIDIAN

### History → Transformation → Emergence

**MERIDIAN** is an architectural research and design project investigating how historical knowledge, architectural evidence, spatial systems, formal systems, and cultural inheritance can be extracted, transformed, recombined, and synthesized into a contemporary architectural language.

It is not conceived as a reproduction of historical architecture.

It is an investigation into transformation.

# 01 — PROJECT

MERIDIAN begins with a fundamental question:

> How can historical architecture become an active source of transformation rather than an object of reproduction?

The project investigates the transition from historical knowledge to contemporary architectural production through a structured process of:

**Observation → Extraction → Transformation → Synthesis → Emergence**

The objective is not to imitate historical forms, but to investigate the systems, relationships, forces, spatial logics, cultural conditions, and architectural knowledge embedded within them.

---

# 02 — CORE PROPOSITION

MERIDIAN is built around three fundamental operations:

### HISTORY

Historical architecture is treated as a field of evidence, knowledge, systems, relationships, and encoded architectural intelligence.

### TRANSFORMATION

Extracted knowledge is subjected to operations, protocols, reinterpretations, combinations, and controlled mutations.

### EMERGENCE

The resulting relationships generate new spatial, formal, material, atmospheric, and architectural possibilities.

**History → Transformation → Emergence**

This sequence forms the conceptual backbone of the project.

---

# 03 — THE ARCHITECTURAL ARTIFACT

At the center of MERIDIAN is a physical architectural proposition.

The physical artifact is not treated as an isolated building.

It functions simultaneously as:

- architectural proposition
- experimental field
- synthesis of transformed historical knowledge
- spatial argument
- physical evidence
- research artifact
- test of the methodology

The architecture is therefore both **result and evidence**.

# 04 — RESEARCH

The research layer investigates historical, architectural, cultural, spatial, formal, technical, theoretical, and interdisciplinary sources relevant to the project.

Research is not treated as background decoration.

Sources become operational material.

The project therefore distinguishes between:

- Sources
- Evidence
- Observations
- Interpretations
- Extracted principles
- Hypotheses
- Transformations
- Design decisions
- Rejected paths
- Emergent results

Research records are progressively connected to the design process.

# 05 — DESIGN METHODOLOGY

MERIDIAN develops a design methodology through which historical knowledge can be transformed into contemporary architectural systems.

The methodology investigates:

- extraction
- abstraction
- transformation
- recombination
- synthesis
- spatial systems
- formal systems
- material relationships
- light and shadow
- time
- movement
- geometry
- proportion
- structural logic
- environmental relationships
- cultural translation
- emergence

The objective is to make the transformation process traceable without reducing architectural design to a mechanical formula.

# 06 — THE FIVE DOSSIERS

MERIDIAN is organized through five interconnected research and design dossiers.

### DOSSIER I
## RESEARCH / INVESTIGATION

The research laboratory.

Sources, evidence, historical investigation, questions, discoveries, extraction, analysis, hypotheses, and research methodology.

---

### DOSSIER II
## DESIGN / CONSTRUCTION

The transformation engine.

Research is converted into design operations, protocols, systems, diagrams, spatial logic, formal logic, synthesis, and architectural decisions.

---

### DOSSIER III
## PAPER / PUBLIC ARGUMENT

The formal scholarly and architectural argument.

This dossier establishes the research question, theoretical framework, methodology, findings, discussion, limitations, contribution, and references of the project.

---

### DOSSIER IV
## DARK FOREST

The exploratory and unresolved layer of MERIDIAN.

It contains alternative paths, hidden relationships, rejected hypotheses, unresolved questions, experiments, unexpected connections, speculative interpretations, and research opportunities.

The Dark Forest is not a collection of secrets.

It is a field of possibilities.

---

### DOSSIER V
## MERIDIAN CORE

The evolving knowledge and architectural ecosystem surrounding the project.

The Core is designed as a potential framework through which future researchers, architects, students, and collaborators can investigate, extend, reinterpret, test, or fork aspects of the MERIDIAN methodology.

# 10 — RESEARCH PRINCIPLE

MERIDIAN does not seek to establish a single unquestionable interpretation of architectural history.

Instead, it seeks to construct a rigorous environment in which historical knowledge can be examined, challenged, transformed, recombined, tested, and translated into architectural propositions.

Evidence and interpretation are therefore distinguished throughout the research.

---

# 11 — DOCUMENTATION PRINCIPLE

Every significant artifact should remain connected to its origin and consequences.

Where applicable, documentation records:

**Origin → Evidence → Interpretation → Operation → Transformation → Decision → Result**

This allows the project to remain intellectually traceable while preserving room for architectural intuition, experimentation, and emergence.
