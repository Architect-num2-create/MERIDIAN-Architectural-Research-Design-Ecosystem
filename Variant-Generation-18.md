# 18 — VARIANT GENERATION

Variants are not different costumes for the same building.

They are different answers to the same architectural problem.

---

Take one unresolved question:

**How should the centre relate to the surrounding field?**

Now make real alternatives.

### A
The centre dominates.

### B
The centre is partially absorbed.

### C
The centre is hidden until arrival.

### D
The centre is broken into several linked spaces.

### E
The centre remains geometrically clear but spatially ambiguous.

These are not five shapes.

They are five architectural propositions.

---

## Then test them in plan and section.

The same variant may look strong in plan and fail in section.

Another may look strange from outside and become much stronger when entered.

That is exactly why variants matter.

---

## Never change everything at once.

If mass, centre, dome, openings, material and structure all change together, we learn almost nothing.

Change a few variables.

Keep the rest stable.

Then the difference becomes readable.

---

## A good variant should teach something.

It might reveal:

the centre wants more distance.

the peripheral masses are too repetitive.

the split creates a better threshold.

the dome needs a larger interior volume.

the radial geometry works in plan but not in section.

the historical reference becomes stronger when its visible form is removed.

That last one is especially important.

Sometimes the best variant is the one that looks **less historical** but carries more of the historical intelligence.

---

## Keep the failures.

A failed variant can tell us more than a successful one.

Especially when we can say exactly why it failed.

"Ugly" is not enough.

Too shallow.
Too repetitive.
No hierarchy.
Weak threshold.
Dead centre.
Unconvincing structure.
Light has nowhere to go.
Geometry has no spatial consequence.

Those are useful failures.
