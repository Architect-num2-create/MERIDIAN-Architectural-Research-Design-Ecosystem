FOREST PROTOCOLS

These are the working protocols for putting real material into Dark Forest.

They are deliberately simple.

If the protocol becomes harder to use than the investigation itself, the protocol is wrong.

---

PROTOCOL 01 — ENTER

Use this when something enters the Forest.

Record:

Name

Give it a short name that another person can understand.

Origin

Where did it come from?

Examples:

- MERIDIAN drawing
- historical source
- research note
- design review
- failed experiment
- conversation
- observation
- external field
- unexpected result

First observation

What was actually noticed?

One or two sentences.

Why it matters

What could change if this turns out to be important?

Current state

Choose one:

- observation
- question
- hypothesis
- contradiction
- failure
- seed
- branch
- experiment
- unresolved

Do not make it sound more certain than it is.

---

PROTOCOL 02 — SPLIT

Use this when one question produces multiple possible explanations.

Write the original question first.

Then create separate branches.

For each branch:

Claim

What is this branch saying?

Why it could be true

What makes it worth following?

What it explains

What does it explain better than the current interpretation?

What it cannot explain

Where does it struggle?

What would test it

What evidence or experiment could distinguish it from the others?

Branches should not be merged simply because they are inconvenient to maintain separately.

---

PROTOCOL 03 — ATTACK

Use this for an idea that currently feels convincing.

Try to break it.

Ask:

1. What assumption is carrying the argument?
2. What evidence is missing?
3. What alternative explanation fits?
4. What evidence would contradict the idea?
5. Are we confusing correlation with causation?
6. Are we projecting contemporary ideas onto historical material?
7. Did the conclusion exist before the evidence was collected?
8. Would another researcher interpret the same material differently?

Write the strongest attack you can.

Then record whether the idea survived.

---

PROTOCOL 04 — TEST

A test needs a prediction.

Not:

«"Let's investigate this."»

Instead:

If X is true, we should observe Y.

Then define:

Test

What are we actually doing?

Expected result

What would support the idea?

Contradictory result

What would weaken or reject it?

Actual result

What happened?

Next move

What happens now?

A test can be tiny.

A drawing, calculation, comparison, model, simulation, site observation, reading experiment, or conversation can all be tests if the expected outcome is clear enough.

---

PROTOCOL 05 — FAIL

When something fails, do not immediately replace it.

Record it.

What we tried

Describe the actual attempt.

What we expected

What should have happened?

What happened instead

Describe the result without defending it.

Why it might have failed

Separate known causes from guesses.

What the failure changes

Did it kill the idea?

Did it modify it?

Did it expose a new problem?

What survives

Sometimes the original idea fails but one part remains useful.

Keep that part.

---

PROTOCOL 06 — COUNTERFACTUAL

Change one important condition.

Examples:

- remove a historical source
- reverse an assumption
- change a structural constraint
- change the scale
- remove a program
- assume the historical interpretation is wrong
- assume the pattern is accidental
- assume the opposite relationship

Then ask:

What changes?

What survives?

What collapses?

What new possibility appears?

The purpose is not imagination for its own sake.

The purpose is to discover dependencies.

---

PROTOCOL 07 — SEED

Use this when something is not strong enough to become research but is too valuable to lose.

A seed needs only:

Observation

Question

Possible direction

Why it might matter

What could grow it

Do not force a seed into a conclusion.

A seed is allowed to stay small.

---

PROTOCOL 08 — CROSS-FIELD

When connecting architecture to another field, record both sides.

Architectural problem

What are we actually trying to understand?

External concept

What does the other field actually study?

Real overlap

Where do the two genuinely touch?

Risk of false connection

Why might the analogy be misleading?

Test

What could determine whether the connection is useful?

This prevents "neuroscience says..." from becoming a shortcut for a spatial argument.

---

PROTOCOL 09 — RESURRECT

A terminated branch can return.

But it needs a reason.

Record:

Why it was killed

What new information appeared

Why the new information changes the situation

What part of the old branch is being recovered

Do not resurrect an idea simply because you like it again.

---

PROTOCOL 10 — TERMINATE

A branch can be terminated when:

- evidence strongly contradicts it
- the required assumption is no longer defensible
- testing repeatedly fails
- a better explanation accounts for the same material
- the branch produces no useful consequence
- continuing would only create noise

Termination is not deletion.

Keep the record.

---

PROTOCOL 11 — MOVE

Material should move when its role changes.

Examples:

Forest → Research

when a question becomes a researchable problem.

Forest → Design

when a tested insight becomes a design input.

Research → Forest

when evidence creates an unresolved contradiction.

Design → Forest

when a design experiment produces an unexpected result.

Forest → Paper

when an argument becomes sufficiently supported and bounded.

The original trace should remain.

---

PROTOCOL 12 — CHANGE

When an object changes substantially, do not silently overwrite its history.

Record:

Before

What did we think?

Trigger

What caused the change?

After

What do we think now?

What remains uncertain

What did the change not solve?

This is especially important when a polished conclusion replaces a messy earlier interpretation.

---

PROTOCOL 13 — FUTURE READER

Before closing an important investigation, ask:

«Could someone who was not present understand why we ended here?»

If not, add:

- missing context
- key evidence
- discarded alternatives
- important failures
- unresolved questions

Do not write a heroic story.

Write a usable trail.

---

PROTOCOL 14 — NOISE CHECK

Every few months, inspect active Forest material.

For each object ask:

Is it still alive?

If yes, why?

If no:

- terminate it
- archive it
- merge it
- move it
- or explain why it remains unresolved

The Forest should grow.

It should also metabolize.

---

Minimum record

Not every object needs a huge document.

At minimum:

NAME
ORIGIN
OBSERVATION
WHY IT MATTERS
CURRENT STATE
NEXT QUESTION

Everything else is added when the object earns it.

---

Final protocol

Before publishing anything from Dark Forest elsewhere:

Check the evidence.

Check the provenance.

Check the alternatives.

Check whether the conclusion is stronger than the material allows.

Then publish the strongest version that survives.

Not the strongest version that sounds impressive.
