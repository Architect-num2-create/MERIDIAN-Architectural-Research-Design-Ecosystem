# MERIDIAN — Architectural Variables

This is where the abstract DNA touches actual architecture.

I would watch a small group of variables very closely.

**Mass ↔ Void**

The building is not only made by adding volume.

Some of its identity may come from what is removed.

The real test is whether the void creates another spatial condition rather than just another shape in plan.

**Centre ↔ Periphery**

There is a dominant central condition and a differentiated surrounding field.

The relationship can be changed without destroying the idea.

The centre can become heavier.

The periphery can become more active.

Or the two can approach each other until the hierarchy becomes unstable.

That instability may itself produce a useful design.

**Axis ↔ Radial**

The project already carries both.

The axial logic gives direction.

The radial logic gives convergence.

The tension between them is potentially more valuable than choosing one.

A person can approach axially and then enter a radial space.

A structural grid can begin orthogonally and resolve radially.

A roof can do something different again.

The important thing is that the systems meet somewhere.

**Layer ↔ Exposure**

The project repeatedly works with layers and then reveals them.

That can happen in plan, section, façade, threshold or light.

A deep entrance is one obvious place.

But the same intelligence could appear elsewhere.

**Unity ↔ Difference**

This may be the central architectural tension.

If everything becomes different, the building fragments.

If everything becomes the same, transformation disappears.

The design lives in the interval.

**Weight ↔ Lightness**

Monumental architecture often becomes heavy through mass, depth and shadow.

But the project also wants change, openings, light and emergence.

The useful question is how a heavy building can contain moments of release without becoming visually lightweight.

**Surface ↔ Depth**

Pattern should not sit only on the surface.

A pattern can follow a structural bay.

A relief can deepen a threshold.

A striation can alter light.

A colour field can mark a spatial hierarchy.

Then surface becomes depth.

---

These variables are not a checklist.

They are the places where design decisions can become consequential.
