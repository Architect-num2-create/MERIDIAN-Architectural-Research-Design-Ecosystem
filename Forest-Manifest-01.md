FOREST MANIFEST

Why this place exists

MERIDIAN needs somewhere where its own ideas can be attacked.

Research can become too focused on proving things.

Design can become too focused on making things work.

A paper has to eventually choose an argument.

Dark Forest does not.

It exists before the answer.

This is where MERIDIAN can say:

«Maybe we are wrong.»

And then actually do something about it.

---

The Forest is not a mood

"Dark" does not mean mysterious.

"Forest" does not mean a pretty metaphor for complexity.

The name is useful because a forest does not give you one clean path.

You enter somewhere.

You notice something.

You follow it.

It splits.

One path dies.

Another becomes more interesting.

Something you ignored turns out to matter.

You come back.

You find another route.

That is how this dossier should behave.

The name is only useful if the work behaves accordingly.

---

Five commitments

01 — Keep uncertainty visible

Never upgrade an idea because it sounds better.

If it is a fact, show the basis.

If it is an interpretation, say so.

If it is a hypothesis, keep it a hypothesis.

If it is an intuition, let it remain an intuition until there is a reason to move it.

The Forest should make uncertainty easier to work with, not easier to hide.

---

02 — Attack your own ideas

Every important idea should eventually meet resistance.

Not because every idea is bad.

Because an idea that has never met resistance tells us very little about itself.

Ask:

- What would make this wrong?
- What am I assuming?
- What alternative explanation fits the same evidence?
- What happens if the opposite is true?
- What part of the idea depends on something that has not been checked?

If an idea survives, it becomes stronger.

If it fails, we have learned something.

Both outcomes are useful.

---

03 — Follow anomalies

A strange thing is often more useful than a clean thing.

If one measurement does not fit.

If one building behaves differently.

If two sources tell different stories.

If a design produces an unexpected result.

Do not immediately remove the anomaly.

Stop.

Look at it.

Ask why.

The Forest gives anomalies time to become questions.

---

04 — Let branches grow

Do not kill an alternative simply because it is not the current direction.

Keep good alternatives alive long enough to compare them.

But do not let every possibility live forever either.

A branch needs a reason to stay alive.

It may survive because:

- evidence supports it
- it explains something unusually well
- it opens a useful design possibility
- it exposes a weakness in the current model
- it connects previously unrelated material

Otherwise it can sleep, terminate, or disappear from active work.

---

05 — Make everything useful eventually

The Forest is allowed to contain unfinished material.

It is not allowed to become a landfill.

A useful object should eventually do one of these things:

change a question

change an interpretation

change a research direction

change a design decision

create a new experiment

connect two previously separated areas

prove that an idea does not work

If it does none of these, ask why it is still here.

---

What counts as a strong Forest object?

Not size.

Not vocabulary.

Not the number of citations.

Not how clever the writing sounds.

A strong object has a clear reason for existing.

It can be questioned.

It has a traceable origin.

It knows what it does not know.

It can connect to other objects.

It can change.

And ideally, it makes another question possible.

---

What the Forest protects

The Forest protects several things that ordinary documentation tends to lose:

unfinished thoughts

The idea before it became polished.

failed attempts

The path that did not work.

competing explanations

The things that could both be true until further evidence arrives.

uncomfortable evidence

The thing that makes the current story harder.

weak signals

Patterns that are not strong enough to become claims but are too interesting to ignore.

future possibilities

Ideas that are not useful today but may become useful after another part of MERIDIAN changes.

---

What the Forest refuses

The Forest refuses:

fake certainty

invented provenance

decorative complexity

AI-generated filler

citation dumping

post-hoc rationalisation

forced connections

premature conclusions

dead archives

ideas kept alive only because they took a long time to write

---

A practical test

Before an object becomes part of the Forest, ask:

«If someone deleted this tomorrow, what would MERIDIAN lose?»

If the answer is:

«"Nothing."»

Delete it.

If the answer is:

«"A question."»

Keep it.

If the answer is:

«"A possible future."»

Keep it, but mark it clearly.

If the answer is:

«"Evidence."»

Move it closer to Research.

If the answer is:

«"A design possibility."»

Move it toward Design.

The Forest is not trying to own everything.

It is trying to keep the things that are still becoming.

---

The deeper rule

The Forest should become more valuable when MERIDIAN becomes more intelligent.

That means its value cannot come from how much has been collected.

It has to come from how many meaningful relationships, challenges, questions, failures, and possibilities have emerged from what was collected.

More material is not automatically growth.

Better relationships are growth.

---

Final note

There is no requirement for the Forest to look complete.

In fact, a completely finished Forest would probably mean something has gone wrong.

The project should keep producing things that do not fit.

That is not noise.

That is the reason this place exists.

DARK FOREST is where MERIDIAN keeps the parts of its thinking that are still capable of changing it.
