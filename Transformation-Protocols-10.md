# MERIDIAN — Transformation Protocols

A transformation protocol is not a recipe for generating forms.

It is a way of making sure that an architectural change actually means something.

The basic movement is:

**source → observation → extraction → change → consequence**

The source is allowed to disappear during the process.

That is the point.

---

## Protocol 01 — From historical form to spatial logic

Take one historical condition.

Do not copy its shape.

Write what it is doing spatially.

Then change one thing.

Scale.

Direction.

Position.

Number.

Depth.

Relationship.

Then check what happened to the space.

If nothing important happened, the transformation was probably cosmetic.

---

## Protocol 02 — From one mass to a field

Start with one body.

Split it.

Do not immediately arrange the pieces beautifully.

Let the gap become part of the problem.

Ask what the separation creates:

movement?

courtyard?

light?

structure?

hierarchy?

Then aggregate again.

The final composition should carry evidence of the split even if the split is no longer visually obvious.

---

## Protocol 03 — Axis meets radial

Start with the axial organization.

Introduce radial organization at one precise moment.

Not everywhere.

Find where the two systems need each other.

That point can become a major architectural event.

It might be the centre.

It might be a threshold.

It might be a structural transition.

It might be a change in section.

---

## Protocol 04 — Make the dome earn its form

Start with the simplest dome.

Then introduce the step / striation / deformation.

After every change ask:

what changed in section?

what changed in structure?

what changed in light?

what changed inside?

If the answer is only "it looks more interesting", stop.

---

## Protocol 05 — Translate instead of decorate

Take an Iranian geometric or spatial condition.

Remove its visible pattern.

Keep the relationship.

Put that relationship somewhere else:

threshold,

section,

structure,

floor,

light,

or circulation.

Only after that should surface language return.

---

## Protocol 06 — Allow emergence

Sometimes the transformed result will not resemble the original intention.

Do not fix it immediately.

First ask whether the new condition is architecturally stronger.

If it is stronger, follow it.

If it is only strange, reject it.

Unexpected is not the same as good.

---

## The final test

Every transformation worth keeping should survive three questions:

**What was changed?**

**Why was it changed?**

**What did the building become because of it?**

The third question is the one that matters most.
