# 15 — MATERIAL & CONSTRUCTION GRAMMAR

Material should be where the large architectural idea becomes touchable.

Not decoration.
Not a final skin.

Weight, depth, joint, shadow, texture, temperature and ageing all belong here.

---

## Start from the architecture, not the catalogue.

The first question is not:

"Which material looks Iranian?"

That usually leads straight toward costume.

Ask:

**What should this architecture feel like it is made of?**

Monolithic?
Layered?
Carved?
Assembled?
Heavy?
Porous?
Precise?
Weathered?
Continuous?

The answer can then lead toward material.

---

## Thickness

MERIDIAN has a strong potential for architectural thickness.

A deep threshold.
A thick wall.
A layered dome.
A recessed opening.
A shadow between two surfaces.

Thickness is valuable because it is simultaneously:

space,
structure,
light,
climate,
material,
and time.

That is much more powerful than applying a historical surface.

---

## Module

If the formal system has repetition, the construction system should be allowed to question it.

Does one geometric module correspond to one stone?
One brick?
One structural bay?
One panel?
One opening?

Maybe yes.
Maybe deliberately no.

The interesting moment is when the relationship is precise enough to be felt, but not so literal that the building becomes a diagram.

---

## Surface and depth

The earlier striated-dome study should be pushed beyond texture.

A stripe can become:
- a rib,
- a structural fold,
- a shadow line,
- a change in curvature,
- a construction joint,
- a drainage path,
- a light filter.

Then the "pattern" stops being a pattern.

It becomes construction.

That is exactly the kind of transformation MERIDIAN needs.

---

## Material and history

Historical architecture can be present without historical material being copied.

A historical intelligence may survive through:

proportion,
craft,
depth,
joint,
rhythm,
mass,
threshold,
structure,
or the way light meets a surface.

The final material may be completely contemporary.

The test is simple:

**If the historical material were replaced, would the architectural intelligence disappear?**

If yes, we may still be copying appearance.

If no, the deeper logic has probably survived the transformation.

---

## Construction should push back

The material system should not simply obey the formal system.

Sometimes construction should force a change.

A joint becomes too large.
A curve becomes impossible.
A step becomes inefficient.
A structural span becomes excessive.
A surface needs drainage.
A thick wall changes the opening.

Those pressures are useful.

They give the architecture resistance.

And resistance is often where the most interesting design decisions appear.
